4. Legal Structure for Lead Generation Partnerships

RESPA Section 8 — The Core Rule

What it prohibits:

  • Giving or receiving "any fee, kickback, or thing of value" for referrals of settlement service business
  • Section 8(a): No referral fees for mortgage applications
  • Section 8(b): No fee-splitting for services not actually rendered
  • "Thing of value" is EXTRAORDINARILY broad — even a $5 gift card can violate
  • No de minimis exception

Penalties for violation:

  • Fines: $5,000 to $25,000 per day
  • Criminal: Up to one year imprisonment
  • Regulatory sanctions

RESPA-Compliant Lead Generation Models

Model 1: Direct Consumer Acquisition (LOWEST RISK — THIS IS OUR MODEL)

  • Generate leads through direct consumer marketing (SEO, paid ads, content, comparison tools)
  • Sell those leads to brokers at a flat per-lead price
  • Price is based on fair market value, NOT tied to loan closings
  • Key: We acquire the lead DIRECTLY from the consumer, not from a settlement service provider
  • This is how LendingTree, Bankrate, and Zillow operate

Model 2: Marketing Services Agreement (MSA) — HIGHER RISK

  • Current legal status: Under heavy CFPB scrutiny
  • MSAs require ACTUAL marketing services with documented fair market value
  • Payments CANNOT correlate with referral volume
  • A $50 lead that becomes $100 if the loan closes = VIOLATION (looks like $50 service fee + $50 referral bonus)
  • The CFPB presumes non-neutral platform placement = referral = violation

Model 3: Employee Referral (N/A for us)

  • Payments to bona fide employees for referrals are exempt
  • Independent contractors do NOT qualify

How the Big Players Structure It (Legal Models)

LendingTree Model:

  • Consumer fills out form on LendingTree
  • LendingTree sells lead to 5+ lenders simultaneously
  • Pricing: $30-$100 per lead (shared)
  • Revenue model: Per-lead fee based on objective criteria (geography, credit tier, loan type)
  • RESPA defense: Direct consumer acquisition through own advertising spend. Payment is for the marketing service of lead generation, not for referral of a specific consumer to a specific lender.

Bankrate Model:

  • Consumer researches rates on Bankrate
  • Bankrate connects consumers to lenders via rate tables
  • Pricing: $100-$250+ per lead depending on type and quality
  • RESPA defense: Consumer-initiated comparison shopping platform. Lenders pay for advertising/placement on the platform.
  • CAVEAT: CFPB has flagged that non-neutral placement can constitute a referral

Zillow Model:

  • Consumer inquires about property on Zillow
  • Zillow routes inquiry to partner lenders
  • Pricing: $75-$150 per lead
  • RESPA defense: Advertising/marketing platform. Lenders pay for exposure, not referrals.

Critical RESPA Compliance Checklist for Our Business

  1. Generate leads through OUR OWN marketing — website, SEO, paid ads, content
  2. Flat per-lead pricing — never tie compensation to loan closings or referral volume
  3. Document everything — services performed, how pricing was determined, marketing activities
  4. Fair market value — pricing should reflect actual market rates for leads
  5. No exclusivity requirements tied to referral volume expectations
  6. Consumer chooses — we provide information/analysis, consumer decides to connect with broker
  7. Neutral presentation — if we present multiple brokers, do so neutrally (avoid "preferred" placement for pay)
  8. License compliance — route leads only to properly licensed originators in the borrower's state

TCPA Compliance (Equally Critical — January 2026 Update)

  • FCC delayed "revocation-all" requirement to January 31, 2027
  • 11th Circuit vacated "one-to-one" consent rule in February 2025 (currently not in effect)
  • STILL REQUIRED: Explicit prior express written consent before auto-dialed calls, prerecorded messages, or marketing texts
  • Consent must be clear, conspicuous, and tied to specific marketing campaigns
  • Retain consent records for at least 5 years (signatures, timestamps, consent language)

Trigger Lead Ban — Homebuyers Privacy Protection Act (JUST TOOK EFFECT)

  • Effective: March 5, 2026 (signed September 5, 2025 by President Trump)
  • Credit bureaus can NO LONGER sell trigger leads unless:
  • Creditor has existing financial relationship with consumer, OR
  • Consumer explicitly opted in
  • Impact: Eliminates one of the most common (and hated) lead sources
  • OPPORTUNITY FOR US: With trigger leads banned, brokers NEED new lead sources. Our timing is perfect. This creates a supply gap we can fill.
  • 74% of borrowers reported unwanted contact after loan applications
  • 66% received 10+ separate unwanted contacts
  • The ban was essentially unanimous in Congress — it's not going away

Lead Generation Licensing in Texas

  • Texas Department of Savings and Mortgage Lending (SML) oversees mortgage licensing
  • Lead generation activities (soliciting) MAY trigger licensing requirements depending on specific business model
  • Many mortgage companies now REQUIRE lead generators to be licensed regardless of legal necessity
  • Action required: Contact Texas SML for a written opinion based on our specific business model
  • Website: sml.texas.gov
  • Alternative: Obtain opinion from regulatory compliance attorney
  • Consider: Getting licensed anyway removes objections from broker partners

Recommended Business Structure

Entity: Marketing Services / Lead Generation Company

  • NOT a mortgage broker, NOT an originator
  • Generate leads through owned media (website, SEO, paid ads, content marketing)
  • Pre-analyze leads using AI for program eligibility
  • Sell leads on flat per-lead basis
  • Price does NOT vary based on loan closings

Pricing Model (Recommended):

  • Exclusive leads, pre-analyzed by scenario: $100-$200 per lead
  • Premium (includes full program analysis + pre-qualification memo): $200-$350 per lead
  • Our leads are worth MORE than Bankrate/LendingTree because:
  • Exclusive (not shared with 5 lenders)
  • Pre-sorted by scenario (self-employed, bankruptcy, high DTI, etc.)
  • Pre-analyzed for program eligibility
  • Include recommended loan programs and wholesale lender matches
  • The broker saves hours of analysis time per lead

Value Proposition to Brokers: "We send you exclusive leads that come pre-analyzed. You know before you call them: what their scenario is, what programs they likely qualify for, and which wholesale lenders offer those programs. You spend your time closing, not sorting."

The Timing Advantage

The Homebuyers Privacy Protection Act (trigger lead ban) took effect March 5, 2026. This is FIVE DAYS AGO. Brokers who relied on trigger leads are RIGHT NOW scrambling for replacement lead sources. Our timing could not be better.

Legal Compliance Framework (Minimum)

  1. RESPA: Flat per-lead pricing, no correlation to closings, document everything
  2. TCPA: Prior express written consent, clear opt-in, 5-year record retention
  3. State licensing: Get Texas SML opinion, likely get licensed anyway
  4. FTC: Truthful advertising, no deceptive practices
  5. FCRA: If accessing credit data, full compliance required (unlikely for us)
  6. Privacy: Clear privacy policy on lead capture, data handling procedures
