1. DSCR Calculation Pain Points

The Core Problem: No Two Lenders Calculate DSCR the Same Way

The DSCR formula looks simple: Gross Monthly Rental Income / Total Monthly PITIA. But the devil is in every single variable.

How "Income" Gets Calculated Differently

Residential DSCR Method (Most Common):

  • Gross rental income / PITIA (Principal, Interest, Taxes, Insurance, Association fees)
  • This is the standard for 1-4 unit residential investment properties
  • Seems straightforward until you realize lenders define "gross rental income" differently

Commercial/NOI-Based Method:

  • Net Operating Income (NOI) / Total Debt Service (P&I only)
  • NOI deducts operating expenses like property management and maintenance
  • Some lenders apply this to residential properties too, which dramatically changes results
  • A property that qualifies at 1.25 DSCR under residential method might only hit 0.95 under NOI method

The 75% Rule Trap:

  • Many DSCR lenders multiply gross monthly rent by 0.75 (75%) before calculating DSCR
  • This builds in a 25% vacancy and expense factor
  • But NOT all lenders do this -- some use full market rent assuming full occupancy
  • Result: Calculating DSCR using full market rent rather than lender-adjusted income (75% factor) inflates your ratio by 33%, creating false confidence
  • Borrower thinks they qualify at 1.20 DSCR, lender calculates 0.90 after the haircut

Vacancy Factor Inconsistencies

  • Some lenders apply a vacancy factor of 5-10% of gross rental income
  • Other lenders apply NO vacancy factor on long-term rentals (the appraiser's market rent assumes full occupancy)
  • Some lenders apply up to 25% combined vacancy + expense factor
  • The inconsistency is the pain point -- a borrower can get quoted by three lenders and receive three completely different DSCR numbers for the same property

Management Fee Deductions

  • Many lenders include a standard management fee of 6-10% of gross rents in their calculations
  • This applies even if the borrower plans to self-manage
  • Other lenders do NOT deduct management fees for residential properties
  • Impact: On a $2,500/month rental, a 10% management fee deduction drops income by $250/month, which can push a borderline DSCR below 1.0

The "Debt Service" Side Is Also Inconsistent

PITIA Components (What Gets Included):

  • P = Principal (always included)
  • I = Interest (always included)
  • T = Taxes (always included, but estimated amounts vary)
  • I = Insurance (always included, but coverage requirements vary wildly -- see Insurance section)
  • A = Association fees (HOA/condo fees -- always included when applicable)

HOA Fee Impact:

  • Condos and townhomes with $300-500 monthly HOA fees dramatically impact DSCR
  • Failing to include HOA fees can drop a ratio from qualifying (1.10) to non-qualifying (0.95)
  • Some lenders also add special assessments to the debt service calculation

Borderline DSCR Ratios (0.95-1.05)

When DSCR falls in the borderline zone, everything gets harder:

  • DSCR 1.0-1.25: Standard terms, but rates may be 8.25-9.00% with 75-80% LTV
  • DSCR below 1.0: Higher rates (9.00-10.00%+), lower LTV (70-75%), require 12-18 months reserves
  • DSCR 0.75-0.99: Some specialty lenders will do these but with massive compensating factors: 720+ credit score, 25-30% down, 12+ months reserves
  • Key frustration: A small appraisal difference, a $50/month insurance increase, or a slightly lower rent comp can push a deal from "easy approval" to "needs exception" or "denied"

Short-Term Rental Income Calculation -- Its Own Nightmare

LTR (Long-Term Rental):

  • Use lease amount or appraiser's 1007 market rent (whichever is LOWER)
  • Relatively straightforward

STR (Short-Term Rental / Airbnb / VRBO):

  • Lenders use either actual rental income history OR projected income from third-party sources (AirDNA, local appraisal-based analysis)
  • New listings: Most lenders accept AirDNA projections, but quality of projections varies wildly
  • Existing listings: Need platform statements (Airbnb, VRBO) and possibly tax returns
  • Traditional lenders either ignore STR income entirely or discount it by 25-50%
  • Some lenders won't touch STR properties at all
  • Key frustration: A $5,000/month Airbnb can be treated the same as a $1,500/month long-term rental by lenders who don't understand STR

Properties Not Yet Rented (Projected vs. Actual)

  • If no lease exists, lenders rely entirely on the appraiser's Form 1007 market rent estimate
  • If a borrower has a signed lease at a higher-than-market rate, most lenders still use the lower appraiser estimate
  • Exception: Some lenders allow actual lease amounts if borrower can prove 2-3 months of collection history
  • New purchases are the hardest -- no rental history, no lease, entirely dependent on appraiser opinion

2. Appraisal Issues

The Form 1007 Rent Schedule -- Make-or-Break Document

What It Is:

  • A document completed by the licensed appraiser as part of the property appraisal
  • Provides the appraiser's estimate of market rent based on comparable rental properties
  • This is an INDEPENDENT third-party assessment -- the borrower and lender have no control over it

Why It Causes Problems:

  1. Appraiser conservatism: Appraisers tend to be conservative with rent estimates, especially in markets with high variability
  2. Limited rental comps: In areas with few comparable rentals, appraiser may use comps that are geographically or physically dissimilar
  3. Lease vs. market rent discrepancy: If a property has a signed lease ABOVE the appraiser's market rent estimate, lenders use the LOWER market rent figure -- the investor's actual income doesn't matter
  4. Time lag: Rent schedules reflect the market at appraisal time, not current or near-future market conditions

When Appraisals Come in Low

  • A low appraisal kills DSCR from both directions: reduces the rent estimate (numerator drops) and may affect LTV (requiring more down payment)
  • Low rent estimate = lower DSCR = possible denial or worse terms
  • Low property value = lower max loan amount = more cash needed to close
  • Borrower recourse is limited: Can request reconsideration of value (ROV), but success rate is low
  • Some lenders allow a second appraisal, but borrower pays again ($400-$600+)

Comparable Rent Disputes

  • Appraisers may use rental comps from different neighborhoods, older listings, or dissimilar property types
  • In markets with rapid rent growth, comps may be 6-12 months old and significantly below current rents
  • Furnished vs. unfurnished rental comps create discrepancies for STR properties
  • No standardized database for rental comps the way there is for sales comps (MLS) -- rental data is fragmented across Zillow, Rentometer, local MLS, and appraiser proprietary databases

Rural Property Issues

  • No clear definition of "rural" -- the same property can be classified differently by different appraisers
  • One appraiser says "suburban," another says "rural" -- and that classification can make the loan ineligible
  • If the appraisal indicates the property is rural in the neighborhood section, OR if rental/sales comps are more than 2 miles away, many lenders will decline
  • Finding rental comps in truly rural areas is nearly impossible -- leads to artificially low rent estimates
  • Some lenders restrict DSCR lending entirely in rural areas; others accept rural with compensating factors

Mixed-Use Property Challenges

  • Appraisals for mixed-use (residential + commercial) require evaluation of both income streams
  • Most DSCR lenders are set up for residential 1-4 unit -- mixed-use requires specialized underwriting
  • Fewer comparable properties make appraisals harder and less reliable
  • Income allocation between residential and commercial portions is subjective
  • Many lenders simply won't do mixed-use on their DSCR programs, forcing borrowers to commercial lending

Appraisal Timeline Delays

  • In busy markets, appraisers can be 2-4 weeks out before they can even schedule
  • Appraisals typically take 7-14 days to schedule plus 3-7 days to deliver the report
  • Total time from order to delivery: 2-5 weeks
  • This single item can blow a 30-day close timeline
  • Rate locks may expire while waiting for appraisal, adding extension costs

3. Lender Overlay Hell

The Core Problem: No Standardization

Unlike conventional/agency lending where Fannie Mae and Freddie Mac set clear guidelines, DSCR lending has NO standardizing body. Every lender creates their own overlays on top of their own base guidelines. This means:

  • A borrower who qualifies easily with Lender A gets denied by Lender B for the EXACT same deal
  • Brokers must maintain knowledge of 10-20+ different lender matrices
  • Overlays change frequently and without much notice

Minimum DSCR Requirements (Vary Wildly)

| Lender Tier | Min DSCR | Typical Terms | |-------------|----------|---------------| | Conservative | 1.25+ | Best rates, lowest LTV requirements | | Standard | 1.0-1.24 | Standard rates, 75-80% LTV | | Flexible | 0.75-0.99 | Higher rates (+1-2%), lower LTV (65-75%), more reserves | | No-Ratio | No minimum | Highest rates, 60-70% LTV, 12+ months reserves |

Credit Score Floors

  • Industry range: 620-680 minimum FICO, depending on lender
  • 620-659 range: Maximum LTV 65-70%, higher rates, larger reserves; some lenders won't touch this range at all
  • 660-699 range: Standard programs available but with pricing adjustments
  • 700+ range: Best pricing, highest LTV (up to 80%)
  • 720+ range: Required for sub-1.0 DSCR programs and best-execution pricing

Property Type Restrictions

Properties that commonly get excluded or face severe restrictions:

  • Log homes
  • Manufactured/mobile homes
  • Properties on large acreage (5+ acres)
  • Multi-unit buildings over 4 units (moves to commercial)
  • Non-warrantable condos
  • Condotels
  • Mixed-use properties
  • Properties in HOAs with rental caps that are already met
  • Unique or non-standard construction types

Geographic Restrictions

States where some lenders do NOT offer DSCR:

  • Minnesota, Arizona, Nevada, District of Columbia, Idaho, Oregon, Washington, Michigan
  • New York, North Dakota, South Dakota, Vermont
  • Coverage varies significantly by lender -- no single lender covers all 50 states
  • Some lenders restrict to metropolitan areas only, excluding suburban and rural markets

Licensing complexity:

  • Some states require MLO licensing for DSCR even though it's a business-purpose loan
  • Other states exempt business-purpose loans from licensing requirements
  • This patchwork creates situations where a broker licensed in one state can't originate a DSCR loan in another state they serve

Seasoning Requirements

  • Cash-out refinance: Most lenders require 3-6 months seasoning; some require 12 months
  • Rate-term refinance: Some lenders require 0 months; others require 6 months
  • Title seasoning: Measured from date deed was recorded to date new loan closes
  • No-seasoning lenders exist but typically with worse pricing (0.25-0.50% rate add)
  • Key issue: Seasoning clock can RESTART if property is transferred to an LLC after purchase

Reserve Requirements (Massive Variation)

| Scenario | Reserves Required | |----------|-------------------| | Standard (DSCR > 1.25) | 2-3 months PITIA | | Standard (DSCR 1.0-1.25) | 3-6 months PITIA | | Below 1.0 DSCR | 6-18 months PITIA | | Short-term rental | 6-12 months PITIA | | First-time investor | 6-12 months PITIA (some lenders) | | Multiple financed properties (5+) | Additional reserves per property |

Acceptable reserve assets vary too:

  • Cash and checking accounts (always accepted)
  • Brokerage accounts: Accepted but "haircutted" to 80-90% of value
  • Retirement accounts (401k, IRA): Accepted by some lenders at 60-70% of value
  • Crypto, business accounts, gift funds: Varies wildly

Prepayment Penalty Requirements

Standard structure: 5-4-3-2-1 stepdown (over 85% of non-QM securitizations in 2025 included prepayment penalties)

  • Year 1: 5% of outstanding principal balance
  • Year 2: 4%
  • Year 3: 3%
  • Year 4: 2%
  • Year 5: 1%
  • After Year 5: No penalty

Other structures:

  • Fixed penalties: 3-3-3 (3% for 3 years) or 5-5-5 (5% for 3 years)
  • Yield maintenance: Most complex and potentially most expensive -- calculates present value of lender's lost interest income based on remaining term and Treasury rates
  • No prepay option: Available but adds 0.50-1.00%+ to the interest rate

Why borrowers hate this:

  • Locks up their equity for 3-5 years
  • Makes it expensive to sell or refinance if rates drop
  • On a $400,000 loan, a 5% penalty = $20,000 in Year 1
  • Many borrowers don't fully understand the penalty until they try to sell or refi
  • The trade-off: Accepting a prepayment penalty can lower the rate by 1%+ -- but borrowers often don't realize they agreed to one

4. Entity/Vesting Issues

LLC vs. Personal Name -- Complications

Most DSCR loans prefer or require entity vesting:

  • DSCR loans are categorized as business-purpose loans
  • Vesting in an LLC is often required or strongly preferred
  • But: Despite LLC vesting, most DSCR lenders still require a personal guarantee from the principal(s)
  • The liability protection is partially illusory -- the individual is still personally liable in default

Personal name complications:

  • Some lenders allow personal name vesting, but it limits asset protection
  • Transferring from personal name to LLC after closing triggers "due-on-sale" clause risk
  • Most mortgage agreements allow the lender to demand full repayment upon any title transfer, including to an LLC

Best practice: Decide on vesting strategy BEFORE closing -- restructuring after is a minefield

Operating Agreement Nightmares

Lender requirements for LLC vesting:

  • Articles of Organization or Incorporation
  • Operating Agreement or Bylaws (required by every DSCR lender even if not legally required by the state)
  • EIN letter from the IRS
  • Certificate of Good Standing (some states)
  • Entity resolution naming the authorized borrower/signer

Common documentation problems that delay closing:

  • Missing signatures from all LLC members
  • Missing membership percentages in the operating agreement
  • Operating agreement doesn't match current ownership structure
  • Missing signing authority designation
  • Entity not in good standing with the state (forgot annual filing)
  • EIN letter doesn't match entity name exactly

Multiple LLC Structures

  • Investors with multiple LLCs (one per property) face exponentially more paperwork
  • Each LLC needs its own complete documentation package
  • Layered structures (LLC owned by another LLC) require documentation for every level
  • Series LLCs are not accepted by many DSCR lenders

Trust Vesting Issues

  • Many DSCR lenders allow trust vesting but require extensive documentation
  • Full trust agreement must be provided (can be 50+ pages)
  • Lender must verify trustee identity, legal authority, and beneficiary information
  • Revocable (living) trusts are generally accepted
  • Irrevocable trusts are problematic -- most DSCR lenders won't lend into an irrevocable trust; those that do offer only short-term financing (1-2 years)
  • Title held in trust must be correctly formatted -- any mismatch causes delays

State-Specific Title Issues with LLC Vesting

  • Some states have specific requirements for how LLC-owned property is titled
  • Recording fees vary by state and entity type
  • Some states require additional documentation (foreign LLC registration) if the LLC is formed in a different state than where the property is located
  • Transfer taxes may apply when deeding property into an LLC in certain states

5. Insurance Nightmares

Cost Difference: Investor vs. Owner-Occupied

  • Landlord insurance costs 15-25% more than homeowner policies
  • This is BEFORE any flood, wind, or STR riders
  • The higher insurance premium directly reduces DSCR (insurance is the "I" in PITIA)
  • Every additional dollar in insurance premium lowers the DSCR ratio

Required Coverage Types

1. Hazard/Property Insurance (DP-3 Policy Required)

  • Must be a landlord/rental property policy, not a homeowner's policy
  • Must be Replacement Cost Value (RCV) -- Actual Cash Value (ACV) policies are NOT accepted by DSCR lenders
  • Coverage amount must equal or exceed the loan amount (some lenders require full replacement cost)

2. Liability Coverage

  • Minimum $300,000-$1,000,000 depending on lender
  • Must name the lender as mortgagee on the policy

3. Loss of Rents / Business Income Coverage

  • Required: 6-12 months of gross rental income coverage
  • If property is damaged and rental income stops, this covers the gap
  • Without it, borrower still owes the mortgage with no income to pay it
  • Many standard landlord policies don't include this automatically -- it's a rider that costs extra

4. Flood Insurance (When Applicable)

  • Required if property is in a FEMA-designated flood zone
  • 30-day waiting period before coverage becomes effective -- must order EARLY
  • Flood policies are separate from hazard insurance
  • Cost can be $500-$5,000+ annually depending on zone and property
  • Flood zone determination can change -- a property not in a flood zone at purchase can be re-mapped into one

5. Wind/Hurricane Coverage (Coastal Properties)

  • Separate from standard hazard insurance in many coastal states
  • Required in hurricane-prone areas (Florida, Texas, Carolinas, Gulf states)
  • Can be $2,000-$15,000+ annually for coastal properties
  • Must be included in PITIA for DSCR calculation

Short-Term Rental Insurance

  • Standard landlord policies contain "business exclusion" clauses that can VOID coverage if property is used for STR
  • Airbnb/VRBO host insurance is NOT sufficient for lender requirements
  • Need either:
  • Commercial hospitality policy, OR
  • Landlord policy with STR endorsement/rider
  • STR insurance can cost 2-3x a standard landlord policy
  • Coverage requirements remain the same (hazard/RCV, liability, loss of rents) but costs are dramatically higher

The Florida (and Coastal) Insurance Crisis

  • Annual insurance increases of 20%+ are common, especially after hurricane seasons
  • Miami-Dade and Broward: Wind exposure and litigation history drive some of the highest premiums in the country
  • Layered insurance requirements: Separate wind, flood, and hazard policies
  • All premiums must be included in DSCR calculations
  • Properties that qualified for DSCR a year ago may no longer qualify simply due to insurance premium increases
  • Wind mitigation reports and roof replacements can reduce premiums but cost money upfront
  • Some investors need to put MORE down just to offset insurance costs and maintain DSCR

Common Insurance Problems That Delay Closing

  1. Insurance agent provides wrong policy type (homeowner's instead of landlord)
  2. Policy doesn't include loss of rents coverage
  3. Coverage amount doesn't meet lender minimum
  4. Lender not properly listed as mortgagee
  5. Entity name on policy doesn't exactly match LLC documentation
  6. Flood insurance not ordered early enough (30-day waiting period)
  7. STR property insured with standard landlord policy (voidable)
  8. Multiple quotes needed because first quote makes DSCR not work
  9. Policy effective date doesn't align with closing date
  10. Deductible too high for lender requirements

6. Title and Closing Issues

Seasoning Requirements That Delay Refinances

Title Seasoning:

  • Measured from the date the deed was recorded at the county recorder's office to the date the new loan closes
  • Most common requirement: 6 months
  • Some lenders: 3 months
  • Some lenders: 12 months
  • A few "no-seasoning" lenders exist but with worse pricing

Cash-Out Refinance Seasoning:

  • 3-6 months seasoning: Limited to 70% LTV
  • 6+ months: Standard LTV available (75-80%)
  • Some lenders allow no-seasoning cash-out, typically at 65-70% LTV max

Title Seasoning vs. Deed Seasoning -- The LLC Transfer Trap

The problem: When an investor buys a property in their personal name and then transfers to an LLC (which they're advised to do for asset protection):

  • Some lenders view the quitclaim deed to the LLC as a NEW "acquisition"
  • The seasoning clock RESTARTS from the date of the LLC transfer, not the original purchase date
  • 8 months of seasoning becomes 0 months overnight
  • Workaround: Some lenders will "look through" the LLC to the individual member's original acquisition date, IF the individual was on title before the transfer AND is a managing member of the LLC

Quitclaim Deed Issues

  • Quitclaim deeds provide no warranty of title -- lenders view them with suspicion
  • Title insurance may not cover defects transferred via quitclaim
  • Some lenders will not accept properties acquired via quitclaim deed
  • If the property was "quit claimed" from one entity to another, underwriting review is deeper and slower

Properties Acquired at Auction

  • Auction purchases are common among DSCR borrowers (buy low, rehab, refinance)
  • Title issues are prevalent with auction properties: unreleased liens, boundary disputes, estate/probate issues
  • Tax lien properties may have redemption periods that affect title
  • Foreclosure auctions may leave junior liens unresolved
  • Title search is critical -- must be ordered early; surprises here kill deals
  • Some lenders specialize in post-auction refinances; most mainstream DSCR lenders avoid them

Properties with Existing Liens

  • DSCR lenders generally do NOT allow any other liens on the property
  • Existing mechanics liens, judgment liens, or tax liens must be cleared before closing
  • HELOC or second mortgages must be paid off or subordinated
  • IRS liens on the property owner are especially problematic and slow to resolve

Subject-To Transactions

  • "Subject to" (buying property while leaving existing mortgage in place) creates significant title complexity
  • Most DSCR lenders will NOT finance a property acquired subject-to
  • The existing mortgage's due-on-sale clause creates lender risk
  • Title insurance companies may refuse to insure
  • Chain of title issues can make these properties unlendable
  • Borrowers who use subject-to strategies often can't refinance into a DSCR loan without first resolving the underlying mortgage

7. Common Reasons DSCR Loans Fall Apart

Close Rate Reality

While specific industry-wide close rates aren't published, the consensus among brokers is that DSCR loans have a significantly lower close rate than conventional loans. Common estimates suggest 60-70% of DSCR loans that enter underwriting actually close (compared to 80%+ for conventional).

Top Reasons DSCR Loans Get Denied After Initial Approval

1. Appraisal Kills the DSCR

  • Rent schedule comes in lower than expected
  • Property value comes in lower than expected
  • Both problems compound: less income AND lower LTV
  • Most common single reason for DSCR loan failure

2. Insurance Costs Blow Up the Numbers

  • Insurance quote higher than estimated at application
  • Additional coverage required (flood, wind, STR)
  • DSCR that worked at application doesn't work with actual insurance costs
  • Especially prevalent in Florida, Texas, and coastal markets

3. Property Condition or Type Issues

  • Appraisal reveals deferred maintenance or needed repairs
  • Property classified as "rural" by appraiser (wasn't expected)
  • Property type doesn't fit lender guidelines (log home, large acreage, etc.)
  • HOA rental cap already met -- property can't be rented, deal is dead

4. Entity/Documentation Problems

  • LLC not in good standing, missing operating agreement
  • Borrower can't provide required documentation in time
  • Signing authority issues delay closing past rate lock expiration

5. Credit or Financial Changes

  • Borrower's credit score drops between application and closing (new inquiries, balances increase)
  • Reserve verification shows insufficient liquid assets
  • Borrower takes on new debt during processing

Condition Stacking -- Death by a Thousand Cuts

DSCR loans are prone to excessive conditions because of manual underwriting:

  • Prior to Document (PTD) conditions: Items needed before closing docs can be prepared
  • Prior to Funding (PTF) conditions: Items needed before the loan can fund
  • Conditions multiply when the file is borderline on any metric
  • Each condition requires borrower response, broker follow-up, and underwriter review
  • Rural properties, non-warrantable condos, mixed-use assets, unique entity structures, and properties with inconsistent lease documentation get the most conditions
  • Late-stage conditions are the killer: Issues discovered after the appraisal returns, forcing program pivots, exception requests, or additional review when the borrower thought they were almost done

Rate Lock Issues

  • Standard DSCR rate locks: 30-45 days
  • Lock extensions cost 0.125-0.25 points PER WEEK of extension
  • On a $400,000 loan, that's $500-$1,000 per week in extension fees
  • Appraisal delays are the #1 cause of rate lock expirations
  • If the lock expires and rates have risen, the borrower gets re-priced at worse terms
  • Some borrowers walk when they see the new rate -- all that work for nothing

Last-Minute Lender Changes

  • Lender pulls back from a program or geographic area mid-process
  • Guideline changes during processing make the loan ineligible
  • Underwriter interpretation differs from initial review
  • The file gets escalated to a senior underwriter who sees it differently
  • Wholesale lender support is inconsistent -- some have dedicated DSCR underwriters, others route to generalists who don't understand the product

8. What LOs/Brokers Hate About DSCR

Why Many LOs Refuse to Do DSCR Loans

1. Knowledge Gap

  • Many brokers and loan officers still don't fully understand how DSCR loans work
  • No standardized training or certification for DSCR/non-QM origination
  • The product varies so much by lender that general knowledge isn't enough
  • Conventional LOs can learn Fannie/Freddie guidelines once; DSCR requires learning 10+ different lender matrices

2. Complexity vs. Conventional

  • Manual underwriting = more conditions, more back-and-forth
  • No AUS (Automated Underwriting System) decisions -- everything is human-reviewed
  • Documentation requirements vary by lender, entity type, property type, and DSCR ratio
  • A conventional loan has a predictable workflow; DSCR has constant variables

3. Longer Processing Time

  • Average DSCR close: 21-45 days (some stretch to 60+)
  • Conventional average: 30-45 days with more predictability
  • DSCR delays are less predictable -- you don't know which issue will surface
  • More time per file = lower throughput for the LO

4. Commission/Compensation Considerations

  • DSCR loans offer brokers higher margins per loan (higher compensation, less competition)
  • BUT: More time per file means lower per-hour earnings if the deal falls apart
  • Higher fallout rate means more uncompensated work
  • The 60-70% close rate vs. 80%+ conventional close rate means 30-40% more wasted effort

5. Lender Communication Problems

  • Wholesale DSCR lenders vary dramatically in responsiveness
  • If a quote/term sheet takes 24+ hours to receive, the whole process will be slow
  • Some lenders have dedicated DSCR underwriters; others use generalist underwriters
  • Underwriter turnover means different interpretations of the same guidelines
  • Scenario pricing is harder to get -- LOs need quick answers on "what if" scenarios for investors

6. Retail Lenders Don't Offer DSCR

  • DSCR is primarily a wholesale/broker channel product
  • LOs at retail banks or credit unions can't originate DSCR loans
  • Brokers need wholesale relationships specifically for this product
  • Building and maintaining those relationships takes effort many LOs don't invest

The Training Gap Is Real

  • Wholesale lenders are starting to offer training resources (RCN Capital has a free self-guided platform; NQM Funding provides educational resources)
  • But adoption is low -- most LOs stick with what they know
  • The LOs who DO master DSCR build a loyal investor clientele (repeat business, portfolio lending)
  • This is the opportunity for Click Click Close -- the LOs who learn it win; the ones who don't, lose those clients

9. What Borrowers Hate About DSCR

Rate Shock

  • DSCR loan rates: 5.875%-8.00%+ (March 2026)
  • Conventional investment property rates: Typically 0.5%-1.5% lower
  • The spread is the shock: Borrowers who are used to conventional rates see DSCR rates and recoil
  • On a $400,000 loan, a 1.5% rate difference = $6,000/year more in interest = $500/month
  • That $500/month directly reduces cash flow on the investment
  • Borrowers often don't understand WHY rates are higher (no income verification, higher risk)

Prepayment Penalty Surprises

  • Over 85% of DSCR loans have prepayment penalties
  • Many borrowers don't fully understand the penalty at closing
  • The ugly math: On a $400,000 loan with 5-4-3-2-1 stepdown:
  • Year 1 payoff penalty: $20,000
  • Year 2: $16,000
  • Year 3: $12,000
  • Year 4: $8,000
  • Year 5: $4,000
  • Borrowers who plan to "BRRRR" (Buy, Rehab, Rent, Refinance, Repeat) get trapped
  • Rate drops and they can't refinance without paying thousands in penalties
  • Yield maintenance is even worse -- penalty is calculated on lost interest income, can be massive

Closing Cost Surprises

  • DSCR closing costs: 2-5% of loan amount
  • On a $400,000 loan: $8,000-$20,000
  • Broken down:
  • Origination fees: 1-3% ($4,000-$12,000)
  • Underwriting fees: $500-$1,200
  • Processing fees: $400-$800
  • Appraisal: $400-$700
  • Title/escrow: $1,500-$3,000
  • Third-party verifications
  • Document preparation fees
  • Hidden fees: If DSCR is borderline, borrower can be pushed into a higher-risk tier adding 1-2% more in fees
  • Combined with higher interest rates: a 2% higher rate on a $500,000 loan adds $10,000/year in interest, plus $10,000-$25,000 in closing fees

Timeline Surprises

  • Borrowers expect 2-3 week closings based on marketing ("close in as few as 6 days!")
  • Reality: Average closing time is 30-45 days
  • Appraisal delays alone can add 2-5 weeks
  • Missing documents add 1-2 weeks per condition
  • Common bottleneck chain:
  1. Application to appraisal order: 3-5 days
  2. Appraisal scheduling: 7-14 days
  3. Appraisal delivery: 3-7 days
  4. Underwriting review: 5-10 days
  5. Conditions/clearing: 5-15 days
  6. Closing docs: 3-5 days
  • Total realistic timeline: 4-8 weeks

Communication Black Holes

  • Borrowers submit documents and hear nothing for days or weeks
  • Status updates are inconsistent -- depends on the individual LO and lender
  • Underwriter questions get relayed through multiple parties (borrower -> LO -> processor -> underwriter -> back)
  • No real-time status tracking on most DSCR platforms
  • When something goes wrong, borrowers often don't find out until days later
  • Lost documents, missed conditions, overlooked emails -- all common
  • The borrower experience is fundamentally worse than conventional lending where there are more standardized checkpoints and automated updates
