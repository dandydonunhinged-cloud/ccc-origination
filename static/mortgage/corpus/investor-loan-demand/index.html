1. Overall Non-QM / Investor Loan Market

Market Size & Volume

| Metric | 2024 | 2025 | 2026 (Projected) | |--------|------|------|-------------------| | Total mortgage originations | $1.8T | $2.0T | $2.2T | | Non-QM origination volume | ~$80-90B | ~$150-160B | ~$200B+ | | Non-QM market share | ~5% | 8-10% | 10-15% | | Nonconforming share of first-lien originations | 16.6% | 20.9% | ~24% (est.) | | Total nonconforming originations | — | $401B | — | | Non-QM RMBS issuance | ~$41B | ~$67B (KBRA forecast) | ~$75B (KBRA forecast) |

Source: MBA Forecast, Inside Mortgage Finance, Kroll Bond Rating Agency, HousingWire

Key Growth Metrics

  • Non-QM market share rose from 5.21% (Jul 2024) to 8.0% (Jul 2025) -- a 53% increase in share in 12 months
  • Non-QM RMBS issuance hit a record $20.9B in Q3 2025 alone -- nearly double Q3 2024
  • September 2025: $8.5B in non-QM RMBS -- single-month record
  • Through September 2025, non-QM RMBS issuance ($52B) already exceeded all of 2024 ($41B)
  • Angel Oak increased originations 33% in 2025
  • Pennymac entered the non-QM space in 2025; Rocket Pro launched DSCR products
  • Total private loan volume: 276,909 loans in 2024, up 14% from 241,701 in 2023

Why It's Growing

  1. Rate lock-in effect: 80%+ of existing mortgages below 5% -- homeowners won't sell, creating permanent rental demand
  2. Self-employed growth: Traditional QM framework excludes a massive segment of qualified borrowers
  3. Investor activity at multi-year highs: Investors bought 30% of all single-family homes in 2025 (up from 29% in 2024)
  4. Secondary market appetite: Private-credit funds hungry for non-QM paper -- coupons 150-250bps above conforming with historically low defaults at conservative LTVs
  5. Institutional validation: Major players (Rocket, Pennymac) entering non-QM signals permanent mainstream acceptance

2. DSCR Loan Demand (The Dominant Product)

Volume & Growth

| Metric | Data Point | |--------|-----------| | January 2025 volume | 4,200+ transactions, $2B+ | | YoY growth (mid-2025) | ~35% higher than prior year | | Lightning Docs DSCR volume growth (YoY through Nov 2025) | 94% increase (15,009 to 29,066 loans) | | Lightning Docs DSCR growth (Jan 2024 vs Jan 2025) | 123% increase | | Share of non-QM originations | ~28.7% (Dec 2024), ~33%+ (Apr 2025) | | Share of non-QM RMBS | ~30% of volume | | Average loan size | $270,300 (Jan 2024) to $312,465 (Dec 2024), normalized to $281,048 (Jan 2025) | | Typical loan range | $100K - $3M |

Source: HousingWire, AAPL Lightning Docs, National Mortgage Professional, RCN Capital

DSCR Has Overtaken Bridge as the #1 Private Lending Product

  • In 2025, DSCR loans surpassed bridge loans for the first time ever as the majority of private lending volume
  • Bridge loans dropped below 47% of year-to-date volume
  • DSCR is no longer "niche" -- it's a standardized, liquid asset class with eager secondary market buyers

Why DSCR Demand Is Surging

  1. No personal income verification -- qualifies on property cash flow alone
  2. LLC-friendly -- investors can close in entity name
  3. Scalable -- no Fannie/Freddie 10-property cap
  4. Rental demand at all-time highs -- rate lock-in effect keeps would-be sellers renting
  5. Institutional pullback -- large investors pulling back from acquisitions, creating opportunity for individual investors
  6. Investor purchases at record share -- 26% of SFH sales in Q3 2024 (ATTOM), 33% in Q2 2025 (BatchData)
  7. Secondary market confidence -- securitization shelves from Angel Oak, Deephaven, Carrington, Verus oversubscribed

  • Cleveland -- Avg rent $1,555, median home price $139,900, best rent-to-price ratio in the country. 26% increase in rental page views YoY
  • Detroit -- Population decline stabilizing, rent-to-price ratios of 0.85-1.0% in working-class neighborhoods
  • Indianapolis -- Median rent $1,395, median home price $270,000, large institutional market

Southeast:

  • Memphis -- Avg rents $1,050-$1,175, no state income tax (Tennessee)
  • Birmingham -- Avg rent $1,353, 43% below national median
  • Atlanta metro -- Moderate prices + rising rents = strong debt coverage; submarkets offer better ratios than coastal cities
  • Charlotte / Raleigh -- Job growth in tech, healthcare, finance driving long-term rental demand
  • Jacksonville -- Affordability + long-term rental stability

Sunbelt:

  • Tampa / St. Petersburg -- Balanced rent-to-price ratios
  • Orlando -- Workforce + STR demand
  • Phoenix -- BTR capital of the US (13,010-unit pipeline)
  • Austin -- Strong rental growth

Source: AAPL Lightning Docs, Jake N Finance Group

Key Use Cases Driving Bridge Demand

  1. BRRRR acquisitions -- Short-term financing for purchase + rehab before refinancing into DSCR
  2. Auction purchases -- Fast close required, no time for conventional underwriting
  3. Portfolio rebalancing -- Investors selling one property, buying another before proceeds clear
  4. Value-add transitions -- Financing the gap between acquisition and stabilization
  5. Construction bridge -- Hybrid products bridging into permanent financing
  6. 65% of successful residential flips in Q1 2026 involved a private bridge lender

Trend

  • Bridge loans are growing in absolute volume but losing market share to DSCR
  • The shift reflects the market moving from short-term speculation to long-term rental hold strategies
  • Bridge remains essential as the "first touch" product that feeds into DSCR refinances

STR Investor Activity

  • STR demand growth of 6.0% significantly outpaced traditional hotel demand (-0.3%)
  • Secondary/tertiary cities experiencing explosive growth: San Gabriel, CA (+709% listings), Davenport, FL (+707%)
  • Average STR occupancy declined from 57% (2024) to 50% (spring 2025) -- suggesting saturation in some markets

The Financing Gap (MAJOR OPPORTUNITY)

Many DSCR lenders remain hesitant to underwrite Airbnb income, creating a significant gap between investor demand and available loan products. Key challenges:

  • STR income is seasonal and variable -- traditional DSCR underwriting struggles with it
  • Many lenders require 12+ months of STR income history
  • New STR investors can't document income at all
  • Hybrid STR Mortgage Loans emerged in 2025 as a new product category combining traditional mortgage stability with DSCR flexibility
  • STR rates typically carry a 0.5-1.0% premium over standard residential rates

Hottest STR Investment Markets (2025)

6. BRRRR Strategy Demand

Popularity: SURGING

The BRRRR (Buy, Rehab, Rent, Refinance, Repeat) strategy has become one of the most talked-about real estate investing frameworks heading into 2026, driven by:

  1. Declining flip profitability -- ROI dropped to 23.1% (lowest since 2008), pushing investors from flip-to-sell to flip-to-hold
  2. Strong rental market -- Rate lock-in effect creates permanent rental demand
  3. Scalability -- DSCR refinance allows unlimited portfolio growth without personal income constraints
  4. Multiple media/influencer validation -- Moneywise, Yahoo Finance, BiggerPockets all featuring BRRRR as "2026's go-to approach"

Financing Requirements at Each Stage

| BRRRR Stage | Loan Product | Typical Terms | |-------------|-------------|---------------| | Buy + Rehab | Fix-and-flip / hard money / bridge loan | 12-18 month term, 10-14% rate, 80-90% LTC | | Rent | (No financing needed -- stabilize property) | — | | Refinance | DSCR loan (cash-out refi) | 30-year fixed, 7-8% rate, 75-80% LTV | | Repeat | Recycled capital into next deal | — |

Refinance Requirements (DSCR Exit)

  • Minimum 6-month seasoning period (most lenders)
  • 25% equity / 75% LTV
  • Credit score 620+
  • Proof of income (property-level, not personal)
  • Max 50% DTI (some lenders don't require personal DTI at all)
  • DSCR ratio typically 1.0-1.25x

Best BRRRR Markets (2025-2026)

Markets with affordable housing, high rental yields, strong job growth, and growing populations:

  • Kansas City, MO
  • Cleveland, OH
  • Indianapolis, IN
  • Memphis, TN
  • Birmingham, AL
  • Columbus, OH
  • St. Louis, MO
  • Milwaukee, WI

Broker Opportunity

BRRRR investors need two loans per deal -- a bridge/hard money for acquisition and a DSCR for the permanent refinance. A broker who handles both sides captures double the revenue per client and builds long-term repeat business.

Financing Landscape

  • Foreign nationals typically need 25-40% down payment
  • Rates approximately 0.50-0.75% above standard (~7.14-7.64% in current environment)
  • Available products: Full doc, DSCR, fix-and-flip, bridge

Broker Opportunity

  • $56B market with a 44% growth rate and limited broker competition
  • Average transaction size nearly $500K -- large commissions per deal
  • Most general mortgage brokers don't know how to handle foreign national transactions
  • Specializing in foreign national lending = high barriers to entry for competitors, high value to clients

9. Emerging & Underserved Segments

HIGH DEMAND / LOW SUPPLY (Greatest Broker Opportunities)

1. STR/Airbnb DSCR Financing

  • Demand: Massive -- $35.75B STR market growing at 10.93% CAGR
  • Supply: Most DSCR lenders hesitant to underwrite Airbnb income
  • Gap: Investors buying STR properties can't get competitive financing; many forced into conventional loans at higher rates or full doc requirements
  • Opportunity: Broker who connects STR investors with the few lenders who do STR DSCR = instant differentiation

2. Foreign National Investment Loans

  • Demand: $56B market, 44% growth, 78,100 transactions
  • Supply: Very few brokers specialize; most don't understand the documentation requirements
  • Gap: Foreign nationals overpay on rates and struggle to find knowledgeable brokers
  • Opportunity: Ethnic community networks, international real estate agent partnerships, embassy/consulate connections

3. Multi-Property Portfolio DSCR (5-20+ properties)

  • Demand: Fannie/Freddie cap at 10 financed properties; investors with 10+ properties have nowhere to go except non-QM
  • Supply: Standardized portfolio DSCR products exist but broker knowledge is low
  • Gap: Experienced investors with 10+ properties being told "we can't help you" by conventional brokers
  • Opportunity: Portfolio blanket loans, cross-collateralized DSCR programs

4. BRRRR Exit Refinance (Bridge-to-DSCR Pipeline)

  • Demand: BRRRR strategy surging in popularity; every BRRRR investor needs a refinance exit
  • Supply: Many bridge/hard money lenders don't offer DSCR; investors left scrambling to find a refi lender
  • Gap: Disconnect between acquisition and permanent financing sources
  • Opportunity: Broker who manages the full BRRRR lifecycle = 2x revenue per deal + repeat business

5. Small Balance Construction-to-Permanent (Individual BTR)

  • Demand: Individual investors want to build 1-4 rental units but lack access to institutional-scale BTR financing
  • Supply: Most construction lenders want $5M+ deals; individual investor construction loans are underserved
  • Gap: The individual developer building 2-10 SFR rentals has limited financing options
  • Opportunity: Niche construction-to-DSCR programs for small developers

6. Self-Employed Investor Bank Statement Loans

  • Demand: Growing gig economy + self-employment; QM framework excludes these borrowers
  • Supply: Bank statement loans exist but many brokers still default to traditional underwriting
  • Gap: Self-employed investors being denied or pushed to higher-rate products unnecessarily
  • Opportunity: 12/24-month bank statement programs specifically for investors (not just owner-occ)

7. Mixed-Use Property Investor Loans

  • Demand: Live/work buildings, retail-over-residential, small mixed-use properties
  • Supply: Falls between residential and commercial -- few lenders handle cleanly
  • Gap: Investors buying 2-4 unit mixed-use can't get standard residential terms or DSCR
  • Opportunity: Lenders with mixed-use DSCR or non-QM programs

11. Strategic Implications for Brokers

The Big Picture

The investor loan market is undergoing a structural transformation:

  1. DSCR is now king. It overtook bridge as the #1 private lending product in 2025. 123% YoY growth. Every broker needs DSCR as their core product.
  1. Fix-and-flip is declining but not dead. Volume down 32% from 2022 peak. Margins at 17-year lows. But it feeds the BRRRR pipeline -- position it as step 1, not the whole strategy.
  1. BRRRR creates a 2-loan-per-deal model. Bridge for acquisition, DSCR for permanent. Brokers handling both sides double their revenue and lock in repeat clients.
  1. STR financing is a massive gap. $35.75B market, 10.93% CAGR, but most DSCR lenders won't touch Airbnb income. Early movers win.
  1. Foreign national is a $56B sleeper. 44% growth, $494K average transaction, minimal broker competition. High barriers = high moat.
  1. BTR is institutional but has a small-developer gap. 139,000 units in pipeline, but individual investors building 2-10 units are underserved.
  1. Non-QM is going mainstream. Rocket, Pennymac entering the space. The window for brokers to establish expertise before it becomes commoditized is NOW.
