1. DSCR LOANS (DEBT SERVICE COVERAGE RATIO)

1.1 What Is a DSCR Loan?

A DSCR loan qualifies borrowers based on the property's rental income rather than personal income, W-2s, or tax returns. The formula:

DSCR = Gross Monthly Rental Income / PITIA (Principal + Interest + Taxes + Insurance + Association Dues)

These are business-purpose, non-QM loans — not subject to Ability-to-Repay (ATR) rules that govern consumer mortgages. Ideal for self-employed investors, portfolio builders, and anyone who shows low taxable income on returns.

1.2 DSCR Ratio Requirements

| DSCR Ratio | Availability | Typical LTV Impact | Notes | |---|---|---|---| | 1.25+ | All lenders | Full LTV (up to 80%) | Best rates, best terms. Sweet spot. | | 1.10-1.24 | Most lenders | Up to 75-80% | Standard qualifying range | | 1.00 (breakeven) | Many lenders | Up to 75% | Property income exactly covers PITIA | | 0.75-0.99 | Select lenders | 65-75% max | "No-ratio" or sub-1.0 programs. Higher rates, larger down payment (typically +5% LTV reduction) | | No-ratio / 0.00 | Limited lenders | 60-70% max | Property may be vacant or negative cash flow. Requires compensating factors (high credit, large reserves) |

Key insight: Choosing a prepayment penalty (PPP) can buy down the rate significantly. A 5-4-3-2-1 PPP can reduce the rate by 0.50-1.00% vs. no-PPP pricing.

1.7 Prepayment Penalty Structures

| Structure | Year 1 | Year 2 | Year 3 | Year 4 | Year 5 | Rate Benefit | |---|---|---|---|---|---|---| | 5-4-3-2-1 (most common) | 5% | 4% | 3% | 2% | 1% | Best rate (0.50-1.00% discount) | | 3-2-1 | 3% | 2% | 1% | None | None | Moderate rate benefit | | 2-1 | 2% | 1% | None | None | None | Small rate benefit | | No PPP | None | None | None | None | None | Highest rate |

Strategy: If you plan to hold 5+ years, take the 5-4-3-2-1 and pocket the rate savings. If you might refi or sell within 3 years, consider 3-2-1 or no PPP.

1.8 Interest-Only Options

  • IO Period: Typically 5 or 10 years on a 30-year term
  • During IO: You pay Interest + Taxes + Insurance + Association (ITIA) — no principal
  • After IO: Principal payments kick in over the remaining 20 or 25 years (higher monthly payment)
  • DSCR Calculation: Uses ITIA (not PITIA) during IO period, which inflates your DSCR and makes it easier to qualify
  • Rate Premium: Approximately +0.25% over fully amortizing rate
  • Best for: Investors focused on maximizing monthly cash flow during the hold period

1.9 LLC / Entity Borrowers

  • Most DSCR lenders allow and encourage LLC vesting — this is a business-purpose product
  • Title must be vested in the exact legal name of the LLC as registered with the state
  • Personal guarantee required by most lenders from the principal member(s) of the borrowing entity
  • Trusts, LPs, and corporations may also be eligible (lender-specific)
  • Operating agreement and articles of organization typically required
  • If property is currently in personal name, can be transferred to LLC at or before closing (title company handles)

1.10 Loan Amounts and Terms

| Parameter | Typical Range | |---|---| | Minimum loan amount | $75,000 - $100,000 | | Maximum loan amount | $2,000,000 - $5,000,000+ (varies by lender) | | Standard term | 30-year fixed | | ARM options | 5/6 ARM, 7/6 ARM available at some lenders | | Amortization | 30-year fully amortizing or IO (5 or 10 year IO period) |

1.11 Documentation Requirements

DSCR loans are no-income-verification — but they still require:

  • Property: Appraisal with Form 1007 (single-family comparable rent schedule) or Form 1025 (2-4 unit income)
  • Existing leases: Current executed lease agreements (if property is already rented)
  • Entity docs: LLC operating agreement, articles of organization, EIN letter
  • Borrower: Government-issued ID, credit report authorization
  • Insurance: Hazard/homeowners, flood (if applicable), liability
  • Reserves: Bank statements (2 months) showing required reserves
  • Title: Clear title with no outstanding liens that would prevent closing
  • No tax returns, no W-2s, no pay stubs, no P&L required

2. FIX AND FLIP / REHAB LOANS

2.1 Overview

Fix and flip loans (also called rehab loans or bridge-to-sale loans) are short-term financing for investors who buy distressed properties, renovate them, and sell for profit. The loan covers both the acquisition and rehabilitation costs.

2.2 Loan Structure

| Component | Typical Range | Notes | |---|---|---| | Purchase price financing (LTP) | 80-95% of purchase price | Experienced flippers get higher leverage | | Rehab budget financing | 90-100% of rehab costs | Many lenders fund 100% of rehab budget | | Total LTC (Loan-to-Cost) | 85-93% of total project cost | Purchase + rehab combined | | ARV Cap (After Repair Value) | 70-75% of ARV | Total loan cannot exceed this % of the projected after-repair value | | Minimum borrower cash-in | 5-20% of total project cost | Depends on experience and credit |

Example: Property purchase = $200,000. Rehab budget = $80,000. ARV = $400,000.

  • 90% LTP = $180,000 funded on purchase (borrower brings $20,000)
  • 100% rehab = $80,000 funded (draws)
  • Total loan = $260,000 = 92.8% LTC, 65% of ARV (under 75% ARV cap)

2.3 Interest Rates and Fees (2026)

| Component | Range | Notes | |---|---|---| | Interest rates | 9.25% - 14.5% | Depends on experience, credit, leverage | | Average rate | ~10.43% | Late 2025 average across institutional lenders | | Origination points | 1.0 - 4.5 points | 1 point = 1% of loan amount | | Document/admin fees | $995 - $2,500 | Varies by lender | | Draw fees | $150 - $250 per draw | For each rehab disbursement | | Appraisal | $500 - $1,500 | Required for ARV determination | | Prepayment penalty | None (most lenders) | Flip loans typically have no PPP |

2.4 Term Lengths

| Term | Common Use | |---|---| | 6 months | Light cosmetic rehabs, quick flips | | 9 months | Standard renovations | | 12 months | Most common. Full renovations | | 13 months | Lima One standard option | | 18 months | Larger/complex projects | | 19 months | Lima One extended option | | 24 months | Major renovations, ground-up conversions, slow markets |

Most lenders offer extensions (1-3 months) for a fee if the project runs long.

2.5 Draw Schedules for Rehab Funds

Rehab funds are not disbursed at closing — they are held in escrow and released in draws as work is completed:

  1. Borrower completes a phase of work (e.g., demo, rough-in, drywall, finishes)
  2. Borrower requests a draw from the lender
  3. Lender orders an inspection (third-party inspector verifies work completion)
  4. Funds released — typically 7-10 business days after inspection approval
  5. Some lenders (Lima One) process draws in as little as 4 business days

Draw structure varies:

  • Percentage-based: 4-6 draws at 20-25% completion milestones
  • Line-item based: Draws tied to specific scope items in the budget
  • Front-loaded: Some lenders allow a materials draw at closing (10-15% of rehab budget)

Important: Borrower must fund the work upfront, then get reimbursed. This means you need working capital or a line of credit to cover costs between draws.

3. BRIDGE LOANS

3.1 What Is a Bridge Loan?

A bridge loan provides short-term financing to "bridge the gap" between:

  • Purchasing a new property before selling an existing one
  • Acquiring a value-add property before stabilizing it for permanent financing
  • Funding a deal quickly when traditional financing is too slow
  • Transitioning from one financing type to another

3.2 When Bridge Loans Are Used

  • Auction purchases requiring fast close
  • Value-add acquisitions — property needs leasing or light rehab before qualifying for DSCR
  • Gap financing between purchase and sale of another property
  • Portfolio repositioning — acquiring new assets while disposing of old ones
  • Delayed renovation — buying now, rehabbing later
  • Stabilization period — property needs time to season rental income

3.3 Terms and Structure

| Parameter | Typical Range | |---|---| | Term | 6-24 months (12 months most common) | | Interest rates | 8.0% - 14.5% | | Points / origination | 1.5 - 2.5 points | | LTV | 65-75% (70% is institutional standard) | | Payment structure | Interest-only (most common) | | Prepayment penalty | Usually none | | Minimum loan | $75,000 - $150,000 | | Maximum loan | $2M - $25M+ depending on lender |

3.4 Rate Ranges by Collateral Type (2026)

| Collateral | Typical Rate | |---|---| | Residential investment (SFR, 2-4) | 10.0% - 11.0% | | Refinance bridge (residential) | 10.5% - 11.5% | | Luxury residential | 11.0% - 12.0% | | Higher-risk investment property | 12.5% - 13.5% | | Land or raw collateral | 13.5% - 14.5% |

3.5 Underwriting Approach

Bridge loan underwriting is primarily asset-driven:

  • Property value and deal structure matter most
  • Exit strategy is the #1 underwriting factor — every bridge loan must have a credible exit plan
  • Borrower experience matters — experienced investors get better terms on complex deals
  • Credit score: Many bridge lenders have no minimum FICO requirement (asset-based)
  • Income verification: Not required (business-purpose loans)
  • Documentation: Significantly lighter than traditional mortgages

Required documentation:

  • Property appraisal or BPO (broker price opinion)
  • Purchase contract
  • Exit strategy (sale plan, refinance pre-approval, or stabilization timeline)
  • Entity documents (if LLC borrower)
  • Proof of insurance
  • Title report

3.6 Exit Strategy Requirements

Lenders will not fund without a credible exit plan. Common exits:

  1. Sale of the property — most common for flip/bridge-to-sale
  2. Refinance into DSCR — once stabilized with tenants
  3. Refinance into conventional — if property qualifies
  4. Sale of another property — proceeds pay off the bridge
  5. Incoming cash event — inheritance, business sale, etc.

3.7 Cross-Collateralization Options

Some bridge lenders allow cross-collateralization — using equity in other properties as additional collateral to:

  • Reduce cash-to-close requirements
  • Increase leverage on the subject property
  • Cover shortfalls in single-property LTV

Risks: If you default, ALL cross-collateralized properties are at risk. Use carefully.

3.8 Liquidity Requirements

Most institutional bridge lenders require:

  • 6-12 months of debt service in liquid reserves
  • Plus projected capital costs (rehab budget, if applicable)
  • Reserves must be verified post-closing

4. HARD MONEY REFINANCE TO DSCR (EXIT STRATEGY)

4.1 The BRRRR Connection

The BRRRR strategy (Buy, Rehab, Rent, Refinance, Repeat) depends on efficiently transitioning from short-term acquisition financing (hard money/fix-flip/bridge) into long-term hold financing (DSCR). The refinance step is where you recover your capital.

4.2 Seasoning Requirements by Lender Type

| Lender Type | Title Seasoning for Cash-Out Refi | Notes | |---|---|---| | Conventional (Fannie/Freddie) | 12 months | Changed from 6 to 12 months in April 2023. Problematic for BRRRR speed. | | DSCR — aggressive lenders | 0 days (no seasoning) | Easy Street Capital, Griffin Funding, Tidal Loans | | DSCR — standard lenders | 3-6 months | Most common DSCR standard | | DSCR — conservative lenders | 6-12 months | Some require 12 months for full LTV | | Portfolio lenders | 3-6 months | Flexible; relationship-dependent |

4.3 How Soon After Purchase Can You Refi into DSCR?

It depends on what you want:

| Goal | Minimum Seasoning | LTV Available | |---|---|---| | Rate/term refi only (no cash out) | 0 days at many DSCR lenders | Up to 75-80% | | Cash-out refi — recoup cost basis only (purchase + rehab costs) | 0 days at select lenders | Limited to original cost basis | | Cash-out refi — based on new appraised value | 3-6 months (most lenders) | 70-75% of new appraised value | | Full cash-out at maximum LTV | 6+ months | 75-80% of appraised value |

4.4 Easy Street Capital Seasoning Tiers (Example)

| Seasoning Period | Cash-Out LTV Available | |---|---| | 0-3 months | Available (no minimum seasoning) | | 3-6 months | Up to 70% LTV | | 6+ months | No seasoning restrictions — full LTV available |

4.5 Cash-Out Refi Rules on Investment Property

  • Maximum LTV for cash-out: Typically 70-80% depending on credit score and DSCR
  • LTV is 5% lower for cash-out vs. rate/term refi at most lenders
  • No limit on cash-out amount (other than LTV cap) at most DSCR lenders
  • Proceeds can be used for anything — business purpose, not consumer
  • Gift of equity not typically applicable (business-purpose transactions)

4.6 Title Seasoning vs. Payment Seasoning

| Type | Definition | What Matters | |---|---|---| | Title seasoning | How long your name (or entity) has been on the deed | Clock starts from deed recording date (original purchase close) | | Payment seasoning | How many on-time payments you've made on the existing loan | Matters for rate/term refi from one DSCR to another | | Rental seasoning | How long the property has been leased/generating income | Some lenders want 3-6 months of rental history for comfort |

For BRRRR: Title seasoning is the primary gate. The clock starts when you bought the property, not when you finished rehab or placed a tenant.

4.7 Delayed Financing Exception

  • What: A cash-out refi with as little as 1 day of title seasoning
  • Conditions: You must have paid cash (or with a source other than a mortgage) for the original purchase
  • Max cash-out: Limited to original purchase price + closing costs (cannot pull equity above cost basis)
  • Use case: Cash buyer who wants to recoup capital immediately after closing
  • Available from: Select DSCR and conventional lenders

5. BLANKET / PORTFOLIO LOANS

5.1 What Is a Blanket Loan?

A blanket mortgage covers two or more properties under a single loan — one payment, one closing, one set of terms. Instead of separate mortgages on each rental property, a blanket loan groups them.

5.2 Key Parameters

| Parameter | Typical Range | |---|---| | Minimum properties | 2 (some lenders require 5+) | | Maximum properties | 20-100+ (CoreVest handles large portfolios) | | LTV | 65-80% (across total portfolio value) | | DSCR requirement | 1.20-1.30 across entire portfolio | | Credit score minimum | 680-700 (many lenders prefer 720+) | | Down payment | 20-25% (of total portfolio value) | | Interest rates (2025-2026) | 7.1% - 8.5% fixed | | Rate premium vs. individual loans | +0.50% to +1.50% | | Closing cost savings | 10-30% less than individual property closings | | Terms | 5, 7, 10-year fixed (with 25-30 year amortization) or 30-year fixed |

5.3 Cross-Collateralization

  • All properties in the blanket loan serve as mutual collateral
  • If one property underperforms, the strength of others supports the overall loan
  • Risk: Default on the loan puts ALL properties at risk — not just one
  • Properties are linked — selling one requires lender cooperation
  • Over 60% of multi-property investors consider consolidating loans a priority (2025 data)

5.4 Release Provisions

A release clause allows you to:

  • Sell or refinance one property without paying off the entire blanket mortgage
  • Remove a property from the loan when certain conditions are met

How it works:

  • Lender specifies a release price for each property (typically 110-125% of the allocated loan amount for that property)
  • Pay the release price → property is released from the blanket lien
  • Remaining properties continue under the blanket loan
  • Not guaranteed — negotiate release provisions BEFORE closing

Important: Not all blanket loans include release provisions. If you plan to sell properties individually, this clause is essential.

6. GROUND-UP CONSTRUCTION

6.1 Overview

Ground-up construction loans fund new builds — from raw or improved land through completed structure. These are the most complex investor loan products, with the highest underwriting scrutiny.

6.2 Spec Build vs. Pre-Sold

| Type | Description | Lender Preference | |---|---|---| | Spec build | Building without a buyer lined up; selling on the open market | Higher risk. Requires more experience and reserves. | | Pre-sold | Contract with a buyer before or during construction | Lower risk. Better rates and terms. |

6.3 Loan Structure

| Parameter | Typical Range | |---|---| | LTC (Loan-to-Cost) | 75-90% of total project cost | | LTV (of completed value) | 65-75% of projected completed value | | Borrower equity | 15-25% of total project costs | | Interest rates | 9% - 13% (private/hard money); 7% - 10% (bank) | | Points | 1.5 - 3.0 points | | Terms | 12-24 months (construction period) | | Extensions | 3-6 months available at most lenders |

6.4 Lot Loans

  • Standalone lot loans: 50-65% LTV on raw land, higher rates (10-14%)
  • Lot equity as contribution: If you own the lot free and clear, its appraised value counts toward your equity requirement
  • Lot purchase + construction: Some lenders roll lot acquisition into the construction loan

6.5 Construction Draws

Funds are disbursed in draws (stages) as construction milestones are completed:

| Phase | Typical Draw % | Milestone | |---|---|---| | Foundation | 15-20% | Foundation poured, inspected | | Framing | 20-25% | Framing complete, rough-in started | | Mechanical rough-in | 15-20% | Plumbing, electrical, HVAC rough-in | | Drywall/interior | 15-20% | Drywall hung, interior work progressing | | Finishes | 15-20% | Flooring, cabinets, fixtures | | Final | 5-10% | Certificate of occupancy, punch list complete |

Draw process: Borrower completes work → requests draw → lender orders inspection → inspection passes → funds released (7-10 business days typical).

Draw fees: $150-$250 per draw inspection.

6.6 Builder Experience Requirements

| Experience Level | Program Access | |---|---| | 0 builds (first-timer) | Very limited. Must partner with an experienced licensed GC. Some lenders accept extensive gut-rehab experience as a substitute. | | 1-2 completed builds | Access to most programs. May need higher equity contribution. | | 3+ completed builds in last 36 months | Full program access. LendingOne standard requirement. | | 5+ completed builds | Best pricing and highest leverage |

Documentation: Photos of completed projects, settlement statements, contractor licenses, permits pulled.

6.7 Conversion to Permanent Financing

Construction loans are temporary — at completion, the investor needs a permanent exit:

  1. Sell the property (spec build → most common exit)
  2. Refi into DSCR (if renting the completed property)
  3. Construction-to-permanent (CTP) loan: Some lenders offer automatic conversion to a 30-year fixed at completion — no second closing needed
  4. Conventional refi: If the borrower qualifies personally

6.9 Target Profit Margins

Experienced builders target 20-30% profit margins on spec builds in strong markets. Factor in:

  • Points and fees

  • Selling costs (agent commissions, closing costs)

7. SHORT-TERM RENTAL (STR) / AIRBNB LOANS

7.1 Overview

STR/Airbnb loans are a subset of DSCR loans specifically underwritten using short-term rental income (nightly/weekly rates from Airbnb, VRBO, Booking.com, etc.) rather than traditional long-term lease income.

Vacation rental properties financed through DSCR programs grew by over 40% between 2024 and 2025. By 2026, STR-specific DSCR programs are available from dozens of wholesale lenders.

7.2 How STR Income Is Calculated

Method 1: AirDNA Projections (No Rental History)

  • Lender orders an AirDNA Rentalizer report for the subject property
  • AirDNA analyzes comparable STR properties in the area: occupancy rates, nightly pricing, seasonal patterns
  • Generates estimated annual revenue
  • Lender applies a discount factor (typically 75-90% of projected revenue) as a conservative buffer
  • Example: AirDNA projects $80,000/year → lender uses $60,000-$72,000 for DSCR calculation

Method 2: Actual Booking History (Existing STR)

  • Borrower provides 12-24 months of actual booking data from Airbnb/VRBO host dashboard
  • Lender uses actual gross revenue (may apply a vacancy/expense factor)
  • Stronger than projections — lenders give better terms

Method 3: Hybrid Approach

  • Combination of actual history + AirDNA projections
  • Used when property has partial history (e.g., 6 months of bookings)

Income Calculation Formula

For new purchases with no history:
75% of AirDNA 12-month revenue projection = Gross Rental Income for DSCR

For existing STRs:
75-100% of actual 12-month average = Gross Rental Income for DSCR
(Some lenders apply 20% expense factor to actual revenue)

AirDNA Market Score Requirement

  • Some lenders require the market to have a minimum AirDNA Market Score of 60
  • This ensures sufficient market data and rental activity for reliable projections
  • Low-score markets may not qualify for STR DSCR programs

7.4 Property Type Restrictions

  • SFR, condos, townhomes: Generally eligible
  • 2-4 unit: Eligible if STR income is documented per unit
  • Condotel/hotel-condo: Limited lenders; treated as higher risk
  • Properties in HOA-restricted communities: Must verify HOA allows short-term rentals
  • Zoning: Property must be in a zone that permits STR use
  • Municipal regulations: Must comply with local STR ordinances (some cities ban or restrict STRs)

7.5 Market Restrictions

  • Some lenders exclude certain oversaturated STR markets or markets with hostile STR regulations
  • Properties in markets with pending STR bans may be declined
  • Rural/remote vacation markets may require additional comps

7.6 Insurance Requirements

STR properties require specialized insurance:

  • Standard homeowners insurance typically does not cover STR use
  • Short-term rental insurance or commercial hospitality coverage required
  • Must cover guest liability, property damage, loss of rental income
  • Some lenders accept Airbnb's Host Protection Insurance as supplemental (not primary)
  • Named insured: Must include the LLC (if entity-vested) and the lender as mortgagee

7.7 STR DSCR Rates and Terms

  • Rates are generally the same as standard DSCR (no STR premium at most lenders)
  • Some lenders add 0.125-0.25% for STR vs. long-term rental
  • Down payment: 20-25% (same as standard DSCR)
  • Some lenders offer 15% down with DSCR above 1.35
  • All standard DSCR terms apply (30-year fixed, IO options, PPP options)

8.6 ITIN Loan Programs

What Is an ITIN?

An Individual Taxpayer Identification Number (ITIN) is issued by the IRS to individuals who need a U.S. taxpayer ID but are not eligible for an SSN. Apply via IRS Form W-7.

Program Characteristics

  • Down payment: 15-25% (some lenders go as low as 10% for primary residence, but 20%+ for investment)
  • Credit: Minimum 600-660 (many borrowers lack traditional credit scores — alternative credit used)
  • Income documentation: Full doc — 2 years tax returns, employment verification
  • Available from: Credit unions, CDFIs (Community Development Financial Institutions), small banks, select non-QM lenders
  • Property types: SFR, condos, 2-4 unit
  • Loan amounts: Up to conforming limits at most lenders; some go higher

Key ITIN Lenders

  • Angel Oak Mortgage Solutions: ITIN mortgage loan program
  • AD Mortgage: ITIN for non-residents — broker playbook
  • Sunrise Banks (Pathway2Home Program): Flexible ITIN down payment options
  • Drake Bank: ITIN mortgage lending
  • Local credit unions and community banks: Often most flexible

9. COMMERCIAL CROSSOVER (5-8 UNIT)

9.1 When Does Residential Cross Into Commercial?

The dividing line is 4 units:

  • 1-4 units: Residential mortgage rules (Fannie/Freddie eligible, DSCR eligible)
  • 5+ units: Classified as commercial real estate regardless of whether the units are apartments

This creates a "missing middle" financing gap: Traditional residential lenders stop at 4 units, while large commercial banks often find 5-8 unit deals too small.

9.2 Why This Matters for Investors

| Aspect | 1-4 Unit (Residential) | 5+ Unit (Commercial) | |---|---|---| | Underwriting | Property cash flow (DSCR) or personal income | Operating income (NOI), debt yield, cap rate | | Appraisal | Comparable sales approach | Income approach (capitalization rate) | | Loan terms | 30-year fixed standard | 5-10 year terms with 25-30 year amortization (balloon) | | Recourse | Personal guarantee standard | Non-recourse options more common | | Documentation | Light (DSCR) to moderate | Heavy — rent rolls, T-12, operating statements | | Closing timeline | 2-6 weeks | 4-12 weeks | | Minimum loan | $75K | $250K-$500K typical |

9.3 Lenders That Do 5-8 Unit as Residential-Style

Several lenders have created programs that apply residential-style DSCR underwriting to 5-8 unit properties:

| Lender | Program | Key Details | |---|---|---| | MMC Lending | 5-8 Unit Multifamily DSCR | Business-purpose loan. Qualification based on rent rolls and DSCR — no W-2s/tax returns. Bridges the "missing middle." | | Easy Street Capital | EasyRent (up to 10 units) | Covers mixed-use and multi-unit up to 10 units under DSCR framework | | Fannie Mae Small Loans | Small Balance Multifamily | Up to $6M. 30-year terms. 1.25x minimum DSCR. Tiered pricing. | | Freddie Mac SBL | Small Balance Loan | 5-50 unit properties. Up to 80% LTV. | | JPMorgan Chase | Multifamily term financing | $500K - $25M+. 5+ units. Institutional. |

9.4 Different Underwriting Standards

For 5-8 unit properties underwritten as commercial:

  • DSCR minimum: Typically 1.20-1.25 (higher than residential DSCR minimums)
  • LTV: 70-80% (Fannie Small Loans allow up to 80%)
  • Debt yield minimum: 7-9% (commercial metric: NOI / Loan Amount)
  • Rent rolls required: Actual rent roll showing all units, current rents, lease terms, vacancy
  • T-12 (trailing 12 months) operating statement: Required by most commercial underwriters
  • Environmental: Phase 1 environmental may be required
  • Property condition: Property condition report often required

9.5 Strategy for 5-8 Unit Investors

  1. Start with DSCR-style lenders (MMC, Easy Street) who treat 5-8 units like residential — simpler, faster, less documentation
  2. Use Fannie/Freddie Small Balance for stabilized assets — lowest rates, longest terms
  3. Bridge into DSCR: Buy underperforming 5-8 unit with bridge loan, stabilize, refi into permanent financing
  4. Consider blanket: If you own multiple 5-8 unit properties, a blanket/portfolio loan may simplify management

11. THE BRRRR STRATEGY: HOW PRODUCTS CONNECT

11.1 The Five Phases

BUY ──→ REHAB ──→ RENT ──→ REFINANCE ──→ REPEAT
 │         │         │          │             │
 │         │         │          │             └─ Deploy recovered capital into next deal
 │         │         │          └─ DSCR cash-out refi (recover capital)
 │         │         └─ Place tenant, establish rental income
 │         └─ Renovate using draw-based rehab funding
 └─ Fix & flip / bridge / hard money loan (short-term acquisition)

11.2 Loan Product at Each Phase

| BRRRR Phase | Loan Product | Typical Rate | Term | Key Metric | |---|---|---|---|---| | BUY | Fix & flip / bridge loan | 8.90% - 12% | 6-24 months | LTC, ARV | | REHAB | Same loan (rehab draws) | Same | Same | Draw schedule | | RENT | N/A (still on short-term loan) | — | — | Lease execution | | REFINANCE | DSCR cash-out refi | 5.875% - 7.00% | 30-year fixed | DSCR, LTV, seasoning | | REPEAT | Use recovered cash for next BUY | — | — | Capital recycling |

11.3 Timeline Example

  1. Month 0: Buy property for $150K using fix/flip loan (90% LTP = $135K funded, $15K down + closing)
  2. Months 1-4: Rehab using $50K rehab budget (100% funded in draws). Total project cost: $200K
  3. Month 5: Place tenant at $1,800/month. Get lease signed.
  4. Month 6: Property appraises at $280K. DSCR refi at 75% LTV = $210K loan.
  • Pay off fix/flip loan balance (~$185K with interest/fees)
  • Cash back to borrower: ~$25K
  • Original cash invested: ~$25K (down payment + carrying costs)
  • Result: Most or all capital recovered. Property cash-flows. Repeat.
  1. Month 7+: Deploy recovered capital into next BRRRR deal.

11.4 Critical Success Factors

  • Buy right: The deal must have enough spread between purchase + rehab cost and ARV
  • Rehab fast: Every month on the short-term loan costs 0.75-1.0% in carry
  • Rent at market: DSCR qualification depends on achieving market rent
  • Seasoning awareness: Know your DSCR lender's seasoning requirements BEFORE you buy
  • Have the exit lined up: Know which DSCR lender you'll refi with before the fix/flip loan closes
  • Reserves: You need capital for the gap between draws and working capital during rehab
