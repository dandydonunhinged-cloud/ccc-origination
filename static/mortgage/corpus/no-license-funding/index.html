1. LEGAL FOUNDATION: WHY THIS WORKS {#1-legal-foundation}

The Core Legal Framework

The SAFE Act (2008) defines "loan originator" as someone who takes a RESIDENTIAL MORTGAGE LOAN application and offers or negotiates terms. The key word is RESIDENTIAL -- meaning loans primarily for personal, family, or household use.

Business purpose loans are NOT residential mortgage loans under the SAFE Act. The SAFE Act was intended to regulate consumer loan transactions and was never intended to cover business loan transactions. (Source: AAPL)

RESPA (Section 8) prohibits kickbacks and referral fees on "federally related mortgage loans." But business purpose loans are EXEMPT from RESPA. Per 12 CFR 1024.5: "An extension of credit primarily for a business, commercial, or agricultural purpose is exempt from RESPA."

Key RESPA Clarification: A loan to acquire, improve, or maintain a NON-OWNER-OCCUPIED rental property is ALWAYS deemed to be for business purposes. The threshold: the owner must NOT plan to occupy the property more than 14 days per year.

Dodd-Frank commission caps do NOT apply to business purpose loans. No 3% cap. No QM rules. No ATR requirements.

What This Means for Don

  • DSCR loans, fix-and-flip loans, bridge loans, construction loans, and STR loans on investment properties are ALL business purpose
  • The SAFE Act licensing trigger does NOT apply
  • RESPA referral fee restrictions do NOT apply
  • Dodd-Frank commission caps do NOT apply
  • 40 out of 50 states do NOT require any license to originate or broker business purpose loans

The 10 States That DO Require Licensing for Business Purpose Loans

The following states require a mortgage lender/broker license even for business purpose loans secured by 1-4 unit residential property:

| State | License Required | Notes | |-------|-----------------|-------| | Arizona | Yes | NMLS required | | California | Yes | DRE or DFPI license, NMLS required | | Idaho | Yes | Mortgage broker/lender license | | Minnesota | Yes | Residential mortgage originator license | | Nevada | Yes | Mortgage broker license | | New Jersey | Yes | Residential mortgage lender license | | New York | Yes | Mortgage broker license | | Oregon | Yes | Mortgage broker license | | Utah | Yes (recent) | Added licensing requirement recently | | Vermont | Yes | Lender license |

2. PRIVATE MONEY LENDERS {#2-private-money-lenders}

How Private Money Lending Works

Private money lending involves an individual or private organization providing a loan to a person or business, often to fund real estate investments. These are NOT institutional lenders -- they are individuals, families, small funds, or entities lending their own capital.

Key distinction: Private money lenders lend THEIR OWN money. They are not intermediaries. This means:

  • Many private lenders are NOT licensed themselves (they don't need to be in most states)
  • They set their own terms -- no Fannie/Freddie guidelines
  • Decisions are relationship-based, not algorithm-based
  • Speed: days, not weeks

Rates and Terms

| Parameter | Typical Range | |-----------|--------------| | Interest rate | 10-15% (can be 15-20% for higher risk) | | Term | 6-12 months (can extend to 2-5 years) | | LTV | 60-75% of ARV or purchase price | | Points | 2-5 points at origination | | Collateral | The property itself (1st lien position) | | Down payment | 10-30% of purchase price |

Do Private Lenders Require Their Referral Sources to Be Licensed?

Generally NO. Private money lenders are relationship-based. Most do NOT require their referral sources to have NMLS numbers or state licenses. The critical legal question is how the compensation is structured:

  • Pure referral (name + number only): Almost universally permitted without a license. You introduce the borrower to the lender and step away.
  • Deal packaging (gathering docs, presenting the deal): This is where it gets grayer. In business purpose lending, this is generally permitted without a license in the 40+ green states.
  • Negotiating terms: This crosses into origination territory. Avoid this unless operating in a state that explicitly exempts business purpose lending.

Compensation for Bringing Deals to Private Lenders

| Model | Typical Amount | License Risk | |-------|---------------|--------------| | Referral fee (flat) | $500 - $2,500 per closed deal | LOW -- simple introduction | | Points on the loan | 0.5 - 2 points (1-2% of loan amount) | MODERATE -- closer to origination | | Revenue share | 10-25% of lender's points/fees | MODERATE -- depends on activity level | | Consulting fee | Flat fee for deal analysis | LOW if truly consulting, not originating |

On a $300K loan at 1 point referral = $3,000. On a $300K loan at 2 points broker fee = $6,000.

6. SELF-DIRECTED IRA LENDERS {#6-sdira-lenders}

  1. Opens SDIRA with a custodian (Directed IRA, Advanta IRA, IRAR Trust, Forge Trust, Accuplan)

8. SELLER FINANCING / CREATIVE FINANCE {#8-seller-financing}

Can Don Facilitate Seller Financing Without a License?

This is the most nuanced area. The answer depends on the structure.

Seller Financing Basics Under SAFE Act

The Dodd-Frank Act provides a seller financing exemption:

  • An individual seller (not a developer/builder) can provide seller financing for up to 3 properties per 12-month period without being a loan originator IF:
  • The seller did not construct the home
  • The loan is fully amortizing (no balloon)
  • Good faith ability to repay determination
  • Fixed rate or adjustable after 5+ years

But this exemption applies to the SELLER, not a facilitator. A third-party facilitator who structures the financing crosses into loan originator territory for consumer-purpose transactions.

Where Creative Finance Works Without a License

Investment property transactions (business purpose) are the key:

Subject-To Transactions

  • Buyer takes over seller's existing mortgage payments
  • Property deed transfers to buyer
  • Existing mortgage stays in seller's name
  • License needed to facilitate? Generally NO for business purpose. This is a real estate transaction, not a new loan origination. But tread carefully -- some states view this as loan origination if you're setting terms.

Wrap Mortgages (All-Inclusive Trust Deeds)

  • Buyer makes payments to seller
  • Seller continues paying underlying mortgage
  • New lien "wraps" around existing financing
  • License concern: This CREATES a new loan instrument. If the property is investment (business purpose), the SAFE Act exemption applies in 40+ states. If consumer purpose, you NEED a license.

Lease Options

  • Tenant leases with option to purchase
  • Portion of rent credited to purchase price
  • License needed? Generally NO -- this is a real estate transaction, not a mortgage. The "option" is not a loan.

Consulting/Facilitation Fee Model

The safest model for Don:

  1. Don charges a CONSULTING FEE to the investor/buyer for deal analysis
  2. Don analyzes the property, structures the offer, identifies the creative financing opportunity
  3. Don does NOT originate a loan -- he advises on deal structure
  4. The parties execute the transaction with their own attorneys
  5. Fee is flat (not contingent on closing) or consulting retainer

Typical fees: $1,500 - $5,000 per transaction depending on complexity.

Key protection: The fee must be for CONSULTING SERVICES ACTUALLY RENDERED, not a disguised origination fee. Document the work: property analysis, comparable sales, cash flow projections, deal structuring, negotiation strategy.

9. MORTGAGE NOTE BROKERING {#9-note-brokering}

What Note Brokers Do

A note broker connects note holders (sellers) who want to sell their seller-financed notes with note investors (buyers) who want to purchase those notes for passive income. The broker earns a fee for facilitating the transaction.

License Requirements

Federal: No federal requirements for licensing to sell or broker mortgage notes.

State-by-state: Most states do NOT have specific licensing requirements for note brokers/consultants. However:

  • California: Requires a Real Estate Broker license to broker notes
  • Florida: May require licensing depending on activity
  • New York: Requires Real Estate Broker license
  • About 11 states require some form of licensing for note brokering

Texas: No specific note broker license required. (Verify with OCCC for latest guidance.)

Business Model

  1. Find note holders -- people who sold property with seller financing and now want cash
  2. Find note buyers -- investors who want passive income from performing notes
  3. Broker the transaction -- match seller with buyer
  4. Earn a fee -- typically 2-5% of the note purchase price

Example: Seller has a $200K note. Note buyer purchases for $170K (85% of face value). Broker earns $5,000-$10,000 on the spread or as a flat brokerage fee.

10. JV / EQUITY PARTNERSHIPS {#10-jv-equity}

The Securities Problem

Connecting investors with capital partners in exchange for a fee is almost always a securities transaction. This is the most dangerous area for unlicensed activity.

Key facts:

  • JV interests, LP interests, and TIC interests in real estate are generally defined as securities
  • The SEC views transaction-based compensation (finder's fees tied to investment) as broker-dealer activity
  • Paying unlicensed finders to refer investors in a Rule 506 offering is generally PROHIBITED
  • No federal "finder's exemption" exists under the Exchange Act

What IS Allowed

  1. Company insiders (officers, directors, owners) CAN sell their own company's securities under the issuer exemption, IF:
  • They were not hired for the purpose of selling securities
  • Securities activities are incidental to primary duties
  • Compensation is not based on securities activities
  1. Non-transaction-based compensation: If Don is paid a flat fee NOT tied to whether an investment closes, this may avoid broker-dealer classification. But this is legally gray.
  1. California AB 667 exemption (2016): California created a limited finder exemption for capital raising in securities offerings. Narrow scope, specific conditions. NOT applicable nationally.

Don's Best Play in JV/Equity

Don should NOT be a finder for syndications or equity raises. The legal risk is too high.

Instead:

  • Help investors find DEBT financing (DSCR, hard money, bridge) -- this is the safe zone
  • If an investor needs equity, refer them to a registered broker-dealer or investment advisor
  • Or structure Don's own deals where he is a principal (GP/manager), not a finder

When Does a "Referral" Become "Origination"?

Under the SAFE Act and CFPB regulations:

A referral IS origination when the person:

  • Takes a loan application (even informally -- collecting income, assets, employment)
  • Offers or negotiates terms of a loan
  • Quotes specific rates or fees
  • Advises the borrower to choose a specific loan product
  • Receives compensation contingent on the consumer selecting a particular loan

A referral is NOT origination when the person:

  • Simply provides the borrower's name and contact information to a lender
  • Provides the lender with property information (address, price, condition)
  • Does NOT discuss loan terms, rates, or products with the borrower
  • Does NOT collect financial/income information from the borrower

CRITICAL DISTINCTION FOR DON: In BUSINESS PURPOSE lending (40+ states), the line between referral and origination is far less consequential because the SAFE Act doesn't apply. Don CAN gather borrower financials, present deals to lenders, and discuss terms -- activities that would require a license in consumer lending -- as long as the loan is business purpose and the state doesn't require licensing.

RESPA: Does It Apply?

NO -- RESPA does not apply to business purpose loans.

12 CFR 1024.5(b)(2): "An extension of credit primarily for a business, commercial, or agricultural purpose" is EXEMPT from RESPA.

A loan for a non-owner-occupied rental property is ALWAYS deemed business purpose.

This means:

  • Section 8 kickback prohibitions do NOT apply
  • Referral fee restrictions do NOT apply
  • Fee splitting restrictions do NOT apply
  • The entire RESPA compliance apparatus is irrelevant
