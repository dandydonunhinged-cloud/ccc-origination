PHASE 6: UNDERWRITING — Full Operational Build-Out

Track conditions, push daily, close on time

DAILY UW MONITORING ROUTINE (10 minutes/day per active deal)

Morning check (every business day, 9:00 AM):

  1. Login to each lender portal with an active submission
  2. Check status: Received → In Review → Conditional Approval → CTC
  3. Check for new conditions
  4. If new conditions: respond within 4 hours (same day, always)
  5. If stalled more than 3 business days with no update: call/email the AE

CONDITION RESPONSE PLAYBOOK

Most common UW conditions and how to clear them:

1. Additional bank statements

  • Lender wants: more months, missing pages, or large deposit explanation
  • Response: Ask borrower for the specific months/pages. If large deposit, get LOE (Letter of Explanation).
  • LOE template:

    • > "The deposit of $[amount] on [date] in my [bank] account was from [source: sale of vehicle, insurance proceeds, tax refund, gift, business income, etc.]. Documentation: [attach supporting doc if available]."

  • Turnaround: Same day

2. Lease/rent verification

  • Lender wants: actual lease (not just a screenshot), rent comparables, or rent schedule (1007/1025)
  • Response: Get a signed copy of the lease from the borrower. If vacant, provide AirDNA/Rentometer/Zillow rent estimate.
  • Turnaround: 1-2 days

3. Insurance update

  • Lender wants: binder with correct mortgagee clause, replacement cost value meets loan amount, flood if in zone
  • Response: Have borrower call their insurance agent. Provide the lender's mortgagee clause info.
  • Mortgagee clause format: "[Lender Name], ISAOA/ATIMA, [Address]"
  • Turnaround: 1-3 days (insurance agents are slow — push them)

4. Entity doc corrections

  • Lender wants: borrowing entity name on contract matches LLC exactly, operating agreement allows borrowing, authorized signer docs
  • Response: Get corrected docs from borrower or their attorney
  • Turnaround: 1-2 days

5. Appraisal conditions

  • Lender wants: appraiser to address specific comp objections, condition items (peeling paint, missing handrails, etc.)
  • Response: If appraisal came in low → file a Reconsideration of Value (ROV) with 3 better comps. If condition items → borrower fixes them + sends photos.
  • ROV template:

    • > "We respectfully request a reconsideration of value. The appraiser used [comp address] which [is not comparable because X]. We suggest these alternative comps: > 1. [Address] — Sold [date], $[price], [details why it's better comp] > 2. [Address] — Sold [date], $[price], [details] > 3. [Address] — Sold [date], $[price], [details]"

  • Turnaround: 3-7 days (appraiser has to review and respond)

6. Credit explanation

  • Lender wants: LOE for late payments, collections, or other derogatory items
  • Response: Get borrower to write brief LOE
  • Template:

    • > "The [late payment / collection / judgment] on [account] was due to [medical emergency / job transition / COVID impact / billing error]. It has since been [resolved / paid / disputed]. My current payment history is clean."

  • Turnaround: Same day

7. Title issues

  • Lender wants: liens cleared, judgments satisfied, ownership chain resolved
  • Response: Coordinate with title company. Most title issues are cleared by the title company, not you. But push the title company to clear quickly.
  • Common issues: old mechanic's liens, IRS tax liens, divorce decrees, missing mortgage satisfactions
  • Turnaround: 3-14 days (title issues can kill timelines)

8. Source of down payment / funds to close

  • Lender wants: paper trail showing where the money came from
  • Response: 60-day bank statements showing the funds sitting in the account, OR a wire confirmation if the funds were recently transferred
  • If gift: gift letter + donor's bank statements (rare on business purpose loans)
  • Turnaround: 1-2 days

APPRAISAL MANAGEMENT

Timeline by loan type:

| Loan Type | Appraisal Order | Report Back | Cost | |---|---|---|---| | DSCR / Residential investor | Lender orders | 5-10 business days | $400-$700 | | Fix & flip (desktop/drive-by) | Lender orders | 3-5 business days | $200-$500 | | Construction (as-complete) | Lender orders | 7-14 business days | $500-$1,000 | | Commercial / Multifamily | Lender orders | 14-21 business days | $2,000-$5,000+ | | SBA | Lender orders | 10-14 business days | $500-$1,500 |

If the appraisal comes in LOW:

Option 1: ROV (Reconsideration of Value)

  • Provide 3 better comps with full details
  • Must be sold within 6 months, within 1 mile, similar size/condition
  • Success rate: ~30-40% if your comps are genuinely better

Option 2: Restructure the deal

  • Increase down payment to hit the new LTV
  • Example: Property appraised at $330K instead of $350K. At 80% LTV, max loan drops from $280K to $264K. Borrower needs $86K down instead of $70K.
  • Discuss with borrower — can they bring more cash?

Option 3: Renegotiate purchase price

  • If purchase: use the low appraisal to negotiate the seller down
  • Many investors do this successfully
  • The appraisal IS leverage

Option 4: Switch lenders

  • Some lenders have more flexible appraisal standards
  • Desktop appraisals, AVM-based approaches, or different comp standards
  • This adds 1-2 weeks but might save the deal

Option 5: Walk away

  • If the numbers don't work after all options, the deal is dead
  • Better to kill it now than close at a loss
  • Be honest with the borrower: "The numbers don't support this purchase price at this LTV."

BORROWER COMMUNICATION DURING UW

Rule: Update the borrower once a week MINIMUM, even if nothing changed.

Weekly update text template:

"Hey [Name], quick update on your [loan type] loan — we're currently in underwriting with our lending partner. [Appraisal has been ordered / conditions are being cleared / waiting on title]. Expected timeline: [X more days to clear to close]. I'll let you know as soon as anything changes."

When you need something from them:

"Hey [Name], the lender came back with a couple of items they need to finalize your approval:

1. [Item 1 — plain English, not lender jargon]

2. [Item 2]

Can you get these to me today? I want to keep this moving so we hit our [close date] target."

When there's bad news:

"Hey [Name], I want to be upfront with you. [The appraisal came in at $X / the lender flagged X / there's a title issue]. Here's what this means and here's our plan: [explain the fix]. I'm already working on it. Let's connect by phone today — when's a good time?"

NEVER:

  • Go silent for more than 7 days
  • Let the borrower find out bad news from the lender or title company directly
  • Sugar-coat problems — be direct, then immediately offer the solution
  • Blame the lender — you picked them, you manage the relationship

UW TIMELINE TRACKER

Track these dates for every active deal:

| Milestone | Target Date | Actual Date | Status | |---|---|---|---| | Docs received from borrower | [date] | | | | Submitted to lender | [date] | | | | Lender acknowledged receipt | +1 day | | | | Conditional approval | +3-5 days | | | | Appraisal ordered | +1-2 days from submission | | | | Appraisal received | +7-14 days from order | | | | All conditions cleared | +3-5 days from appraisal | | | | Clear to close | +1-2 days from conditions cleared | | | | Closing scheduled | +3-5 days from CTC | | | | Funded | Closing day | | | | Comp received | +1-3 business days from funding | | |

Red flags (act immediately):

  • Conditional approval takes more than 5 business days → call AE
  • Appraisal takes more than 14 days → call appraisal management company
  • Any condition sits unclearted for more than 48 hours → you're the bottleneck
  • Title issues that aren't resolving → bring in a title attorney
  • Borrower goes dark → call, text, email — in that order — today
