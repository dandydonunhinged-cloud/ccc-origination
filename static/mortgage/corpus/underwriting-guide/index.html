1. CONVENTIONAL (Fannie Mae / Freddie Mac)

1.1 DTI Limits

Automated Underwriting (DU / LP)

  • Maximum DTI: 50% for loan casefiles underwritten through Desktop Underwriter (DU) or Loan Product Advisor (LP)
  • DU performs a comprehensive risk assessment weighing DTI against credit history, equity, reserves, and other factors
  • Cash-out refinance transactions may have a lower maximum DTI through DU
  • High LTV refinance transactions have no maximum DTI requirement (except Alternative Qualification Path)

Manual Underwriting

  • Standard maximum DTI: 36% of stable monthly income
  • With compensating factors: up to 45% if borrower meets credit score and reserve requirements per the Eligibility Matrix
  • With strong compensating factors: up to 50% (HomeReady program allows non-borrower household member income as compensating factor)

DTI Exceptions

  • Non-occupant borrowers: maximum DTI is lower than 45% for the occupying borrower on manually underwritten loans
  • Borrowers without a credit score: maximum DTI may be lower for manually underwritten loans

1.2 Credit Score Requirements

Standard Conventional

  • Minimum: 620 (traditional threshold, though as of November 16, 2025, Fannie Mae removed the 620 minimum for DU loan casefiles)
  • Individual lenders may still impose their own minimum score overlays (commonly 620-640)
  • Freddie Mac similarly removed the 620 minimum for LP automated underwriting effective November 2025

By Program

| Program | Minimum Credit Score | Max LTV | Notes | |---------|---------------------|---------|-------| | Standard Conventional | 620 (lender overlay) | 97% (first-time buyers) | DU/LP no longer has hard 620 floor | | HomeReady (Fannie Mae) | 620 | 97% | Manual UW: 660 (1-unit), 680 (2-4 unit) | | Home Possible (Freddie Mac) | 620 | 97% | Income-limited program | | Conventional 97 | 620 | 97% | At least one first-time buyer required | | Investment Property | 620-640 (lender overlay) | 85% (1-unit) | Higher reserves required |

Credit Score Model Changes (2025-2026)

  • VantageScore 4.0 now permitted alongside FICO for GSE qualification (announced July 2025)
  • FICO 10T uses "trended data" for more comprehensive credit behavior analysis
  • Both models analyze credit trajectory, not just point-in-time scores

1.3 Loan-Level Pricing Adjustments (LLPAs)

LLPAs are risk-based fees assessed as a percentage of the loan amount. They adjust pricing based on:

  • Credit score
  • Loan-to-value (LTV) ratio
  • Loan purpose (purchase, rate/term refi, cash-out refi)
  • Occupancy type
  • Number of units
  • Property type
  • DTI ratio (newer factor)
  • Subordinate financing

Additional LLPA Categories

  • Cash-out refinance: +0.375% to +4.125% depending on credit score and LTV
  • Investment property: +1.125% to +4.875%
  • Second home: +0.625% to +4.375%
  • Multi-unit (2-4 units): +1.000% to +1.750%
  • Manufactured home: +0.500% to +1.500%
  • High DTI (>40%): +0.250% to +0.375% (newer LLPA category)

Conforming Loan Limits (2026)

  • Standard: $832,750 (1-unit, most U.S. counties)
  • High-cost areas: $1,249,125 (1-unit)

1.4 Income Calculation Methods

W-2 Income (Salaried / Hourly)

  • Use current level of pay (base salary or hourly rate x scheduled hours)
  • No averaging required for stable base pay
  • Verify with most recent pay stub and W-2s
  • Year-to-date earnings should be consistent with stated income

Self-Employed Income

  • Definition: Owns 25%+ of a business, receives 1099 income, or income reported on Schedule C
  • Minimum history: 2 years in business required
  • Calculation: Average net income from 2 most recent years of federal tax returns
  • Add-backs: Depreciation, depletion, amortization, casualty losses, and other non-cash expenses are added back to net income
  • Documentation: 2 years personal + business tax returns (signed, dated), K-1s, profit/loss statements
  • Declining income: If income declined >20% year-over-year, most recent year's income is used (not the average), and manual underwriting may be required with additional reserves (3 months PITI for 1-2 units, 6 months for 3-4 units)
  • Fannie Mae Income Calculator: Available tool for standardized self-employment and rental income calculations

1099 Income

  • Treated similarly to self-employed income for conventional underwriting
  • 2-year average of net income from tax returns
  • Alternative: Bank statement programs (Non-QM) average 12-24 months of deposits minus expense factor

Overtime Income

  • Minimum history: 2 years required
  • Calculation: Average of most recent 2 years
  • Must be likely to continue (employer verification)
  • If declining, use the lower of: 2-year average or most recent year
  • Cannot use overtime with less than 12 months history

Bonus Income

  • Minimum history: 2 years required
  • Calculation: Average of most recent 2 years
  • Must be likely to continue
  • If declining trend, use lower figure
  • One-time bonuses (signing bonuses) cannot be used

Commission Income

  • Standard requirement: 2-year history in same or similar line of work
  • Calculation: Average of most recent 2 years
  • 12-24 months history: May be accepted with compensating factors (excellent credit, strong assets, industry stability)
  • Less than 12 months: Generally cannot be used for conventional
  • FHA exception: Commission income earned for at least 1 year in same/similar line of work may qualify
  • If commission exceeds 25% of total income, borrower is treated as commission-based (higher documentation standard)
  • Documentation: 2 years tax returns + most recent pay stubs + VOE

Part-Time Income

  • Minimum history: 2 years of uninterrupted part-time employment
  • Must be likely to continue
  • Cannot be used if employment gap exists in the 2-year history

Rental Income

  • From investment properties owned: Use 75% of gross rental income (25% vacancy factor) from Schedule E or lease agreements
  • From departing residence: Requires 30% equity (verified by appraisal), fully executed lease agreement, and market-supported rental rate
  • Rental income added to total income; full PITIA included in debts (not netted)
  • Fannie Mae does NOT require rental management experience (Freddie Mac requires 1-year history)
  • Documentation: Schedule E from tax returns, current lease agreements, Form 1007 (Single-Family Comparable Rent Schedule)

1.5 Down Payment Requirements

| Property Type | Occupancy | Minimum Down Payment | |--------------|-----------|---------------------| | 1-unit | Primary Residence | 3% (first-time buyers, HomeReady, Home Possible, Conv 97) | | 1-unit | Primary Residence | 5% (standard) | | 2-unit | Primary Residence | 15% | | 3-4 unit | Primary Residence | 25% | | 1-unit | Second Home | 10% | | 1-unit | Investment Property | 15% (1 unit) | | 2-4 unit | Investment Property | 25% | | 5-10 financed properties | Investment | 25% (1-unit), 30% (2-4 unit) |

Gift Funds Rules (Conventional)

  • Primary residence, 1-unit: 100% of down payment can come from gift funds
  • Multi-unit or second home: At least 5% must come from borrower's own funds
  • Investment property: Gift funds generally not permitted for down payment
  • Acceptable donors: Parents, children, grandparents, siblings, in-laws, spouse/fiance/domestic partner, employer, church, nonprofit, public agency
  • Prohibited donors: Anyone involved in the sale (sellers, agents, builders)
  • Documentation required: Gift letter (stating no repayment required), proof of transfer, donor bank statements
  • 2025 IRS gift tax limit: $19,000 per recipient ($38,000 for married couples) without triggering gift tax reporting

1.6 PMI (Private Mortgage Insurance)

  • Required when: LTV exceeds 80% (down payment less than 20%)
  • Cost range: 0.3% to 1.5% of loan amount annually (varies by credit score, LTV, DTI)
  • Cancellation: Borrower can request cancellation at 80% LTV (based on original value or current appraised value with 2+ years seasoning)
  • Automatic termination: Must be removed at 78% LTV based on original amortization schedule (payments must be current)
  • Final termination: At loan midpoint, PMI must terminate regardless of LTV

1.7 Reserve Requirements

By DU/LP Risk Assessment

DU may require reserves based on overall risk assessment. Common requirements:

| Transaction Type | Minimum Reserves | |-----------------|-----------------| | 1-unit primary residence | 0 months (DU may require 0-6 based on risk) | | 2-unit primary residence | 2 months PITIA | | 3-4 unit primary residence | 6 months PITIA | | Second home | 2 months PITIA | | Investment property (1-4 units) | 6 months PITIA | | 5-10 financed properties | 6 months PITIA on EACH financed property |

Acceptable Reserve Sources

  • Checking and savings accounts
  • Stocks, bonds, mutual funds (100% of value minus costs to liquidate)
  • Retirement accounts (60% of vested balance)
  • Cash value of life insurance (net of outstanding loans)
  • Trust accounts
  • Gift funds in reserve are NOT acceptable (must be borrower's own funds)

1.8 Automated Underwriting (DU/LP) vs. Manual Underwriting

Desktop Underwriter (DU) - Fannie Mae

  • Evaluates comprehensive risk factors holistically
  • Max DTI up to 50%
  • No hard minimum credit score as of 11/16/2025
  • Provides Approve/Eligible or Refer findings
  • "Refer" requires manual underwriting or program change

Loan Product Advisor (LP) - Freddie Mac

  • Similar automated risk assessment
  • Max DTI up to 50%
  • Accept/Eligible or Caution findings
  • "Caution" requires manual review

Manual Underwriting Triggers

  • DU/LP "Refer" or "Caution" finding
  • Credit score below 620 (most lender overlays)
  • DTI above automated limits
  • Non-traditional credit (no credit score)
  • Borrower request (some prefer manual for higher DTI on FHA/VA)

Manual Underwriting Requirements (Conventional)

  • Max DTI: 36% (up to 45% with compensating factors, up to 50% with strong factors)
  • Minimum credit score: Lender overlays apply (commonly 660+)
  • 12 months housing payment history (no lates)
  • Employment stability verification
  • Reserve requirements increase
  • Compensating factors must be documented

1.9 Compensating Factors (Conventional)

The following allow exceptions to standard guidelines:

  1. Significant cash reserves (6+ months after closing)
  2. Minimal increase in housing payment (new payment similar to current rent/mortgage)
  3. Residual income well above minimum requirements
  4. Non-borrower household member income (HomeReady only)
  5. Conservative credit use (low utilization, long history, no lates)
  6. Employment stability (2+ years same employer or field)
  7. Down payment of 20%+ from own funds
  8. No discretionary debt (only housing and auto, no credit cards carrying balances)

2. FHA (HUD 4000.1)

2.1 DTI Limits

Automated Underwriting (TOTAL Scorecard)

  • Maximum DTI: Up to 56.99% when approved through FHA's TOTAL Scorecard (AUS)
  • TOTAL evaluates overall risk including credit, reserves, payment history, and other factors
  • Most AUS approvals fall in the 43-50% range

Manual Underwriting DTI Matrix

| Front-End Ratio | Back-End Ratio | Compensating Factors Required | |-----------------|---------------|------------------------------| | 31% | 43% | None required | | 37% | 47% | One compensating factor | | 40% | 50% | Two or more compensating factors | | 40% | 57% | Energy Efficient Mortgage (EEM) exception only |

Compensating Factors for Manual Underwriting DTI Expansion

  1. Verified and documented reserves equal to at least 3 months PITI (1-2 units) or 6 months (3-4 units)
  2. Low payment shock (new housing payment is same or no more than 5% above current rent)
  3. Minimal increase in housing payment with significant residual income
  4. 12 months on-time rent payments documented by canceled checks, bank statements, or VOR (no late payments)
  5. Significant documented cash reserves beyond minimum requirements
  6. Conservative use of credit with demonstrated ability to save
  7. No discretionary debt (few revolving accounts, low utilization)
  8. Additional income not used for qualifying (spouse's income, documented but excluded)
  9. Housing payment history showing timely payments for 12+ months

Important: One or more compensating factors may be sufficient depending on the underwriter's overall assessment. Not all are required simultaneously.

2.2 Credit Score Requirements

| Credit Score Range | Down Payment | Underwriting Path | |-------------------|-------------|-------------------| | 580+ | 3.5% | Automated (TOTAL Scorecard) | | 500-579 | 10% | Manual underwriting required | | Below 500 | Not eligible | FHA does not permit |

Additional Credit Score Rules

  • All FHA loans with credit scores below 620 MUST go through manual underwriting
  • Borrowers with no credit score may qualify through non-traditional credit (rent, utilities, insurance payment history - minimum 3 accounts with 12-month history)
  • Most lenders impose overlays of 580-620 minimum (even though FHA allows 500)

2.3 Mortgage Insurance Premium (MIP)

Upfront MIP (UFMIP)

  • Rate: 1.75% of the base loan amount
  • Can be financed into the loan (added to loan balance)
  • Example: $300,000 loan = $5,250 UFMIP
  • Partially refundable if FHA loan is refinanced within 3 years

Annual MIP (Monthly Payments)

| Loan Term | Down Payment | Base Loan Amount | Annual MIP Rate | |-----------|-------------|-----------------|----------------| | > 15 years | < 5% | <= $726,200 | 0.55% | | > 15 years | >= 5% | <= $726,200 | 0.50% | | > 15 years | < 5% | > $726,200 | 0.75% | | > 15 years | >= 5% | > $726,200 | 0.70% | | <= 15 years | < 10% | <= $726,200 | 0.40% | | <= 15 years | >= 10% | <= $726,200 | 0.15% | | <= 15 years | < 10% | > $726,200 | 0.65% | | <= 15 years | >= 10% | > $726,200 | 0.40% |

MIP Duration

  • Down payment < 10%: MIP for the LIFE of the loan (never drops off)
  • Down payment >= 10%: MIP drops off after 11 years
  • Only way to remove lifetime MIP: Refinance into conventional loan when LTV reaches 80%

Practical MIP Cost Example

  • $300,000 FHA loan, 3.5% down, 30-year term:
  • UFMIP: $5,066 (financed into loan)
  • Annual MIP: ~$1,600/year (~$133/month)

FHA Loan Limits (2026)

  • Floor: $524,225 (most areas)
  • Ceiling: $1,209,750 (high-cost areas)

2.4 Student Loan Payment Calculation (CRITICAL)

Active Repayment

  • Use the actual monthly payment reported on credit report or documented by loan servicer

Income-Based Repayment (IBR) / Income-Driven Repayment (IDR) Plans

  • If IBR/IDR payment is greater than $0: Use the actual payment amount (must be documented)
  • If IBR/IDR payment is $0: Use 0.5% of the outstanding loan balance as the monthly payment
  • Example: $50,000 student loan balance with $0 IBR payment = $250/month counted toward DTI

Deferred or Forbearance

  • Use 0.5% of the outstanding loan balance as the monthly payment
  • Example: $80,000 deferred student loan = $400/month counted toward DTI

Fully Amortizing Payment Alternative

  • Borrower can provide a written statement from loan servicer showing the fully amortized monthly payment over the remaining loan term
  • If this amount is LOWER than 0.5% of balance, lender may use the fully amortized amount
  • Must be a documented, binding repayment schedule

Key Historical Change

  • Old rule: 1% of outstanding balance (pre-2021)
  • Current rule: 0.5% of outstanding balance (per Mortgagee Letter 2021-13)
  • This change significantly reduced DTI impact for borrowers with large student loan balances

Conventional Comparison (Important Distinction)

  • Fannie Mae: Uses the actual payment if $0+ reported, or 1% of balance if no payment reported or $0 on credit report. Can also use fully amortizing payment.
  • Freddie Mac: Uses 0.5% of balance if no payment reported, or actual payment if reported

2.5 Income Calculation (Self-Employed)

FHA Self-Employment Requirements

  • Minimum 2 years of self-employment history
  • 2 years personal and business tax returns required
  • Declining income > 20%: Must use most recent year's income (not average)
  • Business must be viable (no pattern of losses)

FHA Income Flexibility vs. Conventional

  • Commission income: May qualify with only 1 year in same/similar line of work (vs. 2 years conventional)
  • Calculation: Lesser of 2-year average OR average over time earned (if less than 2 years)
  • Gaps in employment: More lenient assessment of employment gaps with explanation

2.6 FHA After Bankruptcy

Chapter 7 Bankruptcy

  • Standard waiting period: 2 years from discharge date
  • Extenuating circumstances: 1 year if borrower demonstrates:
  • Bankruptcy caused by one-time uncontrollable event (death of breadwinner, serious medical crisis)
  • Event is unlikely to recur
  • Re-established good credit since bankruptcy
  • Current employment stability

Chapter 13 Bankruptcy

  • During repayment plan: 1 year from start of plan, with:
  • All plan payments made on time for at least 12 months
  • Written permission from the bankruptcy court
  • Borrower demonstrates responsible credit behavior
  • After discharge: No waiting period (technically), but lender must verify satisfactory payment history

Multiple Bankruptcies

  • Two or more bankruptcies in the past 7 years: Generally not eligible unless extenuating circumstances can be documented for each filing

2.7 FHA After Foreclosure

  • Standard waiting period: 3 years from completion date (transfer of title, sheriff's sale, or deed-in-lieu)
  • Extenuating circumstances: 1 year (same documentation requirements as bankruptcy exception)
  • If foreclosure was included in bankruptcy, the longer of the two waiting periods applies (typically the 3-year foreclosure period)

FHA After Short Sale

  • 3 years from date of short sale settlement
  • Treated same as foreclosure for waiting period purposes

2.8 FHA Manual Underwriting Requirements Summary

| Credit Score | Max DTI (No Comp Factors) | Max DTI (1 Factor) | Max DTI (2+ Factors) | Down Payment | |-------------|--------------------------|--------------------|--------------------|-------------| | 580+ | 31/43 | 37/47 | 40/50 | 3.5% | | 500-579 | 31/43 | 37/47 | 40/50 | 10% |

Additional Manual Underwrite Requirements

  • 12-month housing payment history verified (no lates)
  • Verification of rent (VOR) with documented payment amounts and timeliness
  • Non-traditional credit may substitute for traditional credit score
  • 3 months reserves (1-2 units) or 6 months reserves (3-4 units) as compensating factor
  • Employment/income stability verified for 2+ years

3. VA

3.1 Eligibility Requirements

Active Duty Service

  • Wartime: 90 continuous days of active service
  • Peacetime: 181 continuous days of active service
  • Current active duty: 90 continuous days served

National Guard

Three pathways to eligibility:

  1. 6 creditable years in the National Guard with honorable discharge or placement on retired list
  2. Title 10 (Federal Active Duty): 90 days of service under Title 10 orders
  3. Title 32 (Full-Time Duty): 90 cumulative days of full-time duty, with at least 30 consecutive days

Reserves

Two pathways:

  1. 6 creditable years in the Selected Reserve with continued service, honorable discharge, or placement on retired list
  2. Title 10 Active Duty: 90 days of service under Title 10 orders

Other Eligible Groups

  • Surviving spouses of veterans who died in service or from service-connected disability
  • Spouses of active duty service members who are missing in action or prisoners of war for 90+ days
  • U.S. citizens who served in allied armed forces during WWII
  • Public Health Service officers and National Oceanic and Atmospheric Administration (NOAA) members

Certificate of Eligibility (COE)

  • Required to prove eligibility to lenders
  • Obtainable through: lender (automated via VA system, minutes), VA.gov (online), or by mail (VA Form 26-1880)

3.2 DTI and Residual Income

DTI Ratio

  • No hard DTI cap for VA loans
  • VA guidelines use residual income as the primary qualifier instead of DTI
  • DTI above 41% receives additional scrutiny but is not an automatic disqualifier
  • Lenders may impose their own DTI overlays (commonly 50-60%)

Residual Income Concept

Residual income = gross monthly income minus:

  • Federal, state, and local taxes
  • Retirement and Social Security
  • All monthly debts (mortgage PITI, car payments, credit cards, etc.)
  • Maintenance and utilities estimate (based on square footage)
  • Child care (if applicable)

The remaining amount must meet or exceed the VA minimum by region and family size.

Regional Definitions

  • Northeast: CT, ME, MA, NH, NJ, NY, PA, RI, VT
  • Midwest: IL, IN, IA, KS, MI, MN, MO, NE, ND, OH, SD, WI
  • South: AL, AR, DE, DC, FL, GA, KY, LA, MD, MS, NC, OK, PR, SC, TN, TX, VA, WV
  • West: AK, AZ, CA, CO, HI, ID, MT, NV, NM, OR, UT, WA, WY

Maintenance & Utilities Estimate

  • Based on property square footage
  • Standard rate: $0.14 per square foot per month
  • Example: 2,000 sq ft home = $280/month maintenance/utilities estimate deducted from residual income

3.3 No PMI, No Down Payment

  • $0 down payment (100% financing) for eligible veterans
  • No private mortgage insurance (PMI) — ever
  • No monthly mortgage insurance premiums
  • This is the single most significant financial benefit of VA loans
  • Savings vs. conventional: A $400,000 loan at 95% LTV conventional would carry ~$200-300/month in PMI; VA has $0

Purchase and Construction Loans

Refinance

| Type | First Use | Subsequent Use | |------|-----------|----------------| | Cash-out refinance | 2.15% | 3.30% | | Interest Rate Reduction Refinance (IRRRL) | 0.50% | 0.50% |

Funding Fee Exemptions (No Fee Required)

  • Veterans receiving VA disability compensation (any rating, 10%+)
  • Veterans entitled to receive disability compensation but receiving retirement/active duty pay instead
  • Surviving spouses of veterans who died in service or from service-connected disability
  • Service members with a proposed or memorandum disability rating prior to discharge
  • Purple Heart recipients who are still on active duty

Funding Fee Calculation Example

  • $400,000 purchase, first use, 0% down: $400,000 x 2.15% = $8,600
  • Can be financed into the loan or paid upfront at closing

Current Fee Schedule

  • Rates set by the Blue Water Navy Vietnam Veterans Act of 2019
  • In effect through 2026 (scheduled for congressional reevaluation)

3.5 VA Credit and Underwriting — THE LOWEST POSSIBLE CREDIT OPPORTUNITY

CRITICAL KNOWLEDGE (most LOs miss this): VA has NO minimum credit score. Not 620. Not 580. Not 500. ZERO. The VA itself sets NO floor. This is the single most powerful fact in mortgage lending for credit-challenged borrowers. Combined with no DTI cap, no down payment, and no PMI — VA is the ultimate YES for eligible veterans when every other program says NO.

  • No VA minimum credit score (VA does not set one — ZERO requirement)
  • Lender overlays: Most lenders require 580-640 minimum — but these are LENDER restrictions, not VA restrictions. Find the right lender and there is no floor.
  • Bankruptcy waiting periods:
  • Chapter 7: 2 years from discharge
  • Chapter 13: 1 year with satisfactory payment history and court permission
  • Extenuating circumstances: May reduce to 1 year for Chapter 7
  • Foreclosure: 2 years from trustee's sale date (1 year with extenuating circumstances)
  • Short sale: 2 years (1 year with extenuating circumstances)

4. USDA

4.1 Income Limits

Guaranteed Loans (Most Common)

  • Household income cap: 115% of Area Median Income (AMI)
  • 2025-2026 standard limits (most areas):
  • 1-4 person household: $119,850
  • 5-8 person household: $158,250
  • Limits vary by county; some high-cost areas have higher limits
  • Household income = income of ALL adults in the household (not just borrowers on the loan)

Direct Loans (Lower Income)

  • Applicants must be at or below 80% of AMI
  • Interest rates may be subsidized as low as 1%
  • For very-low and low-income households only

Income Calculation Differences

  • USDA counts ALL household income (not just borrower income)
  • Includes income of non-borrower adults living in the home
  • Certain deductions allowed: child care costs, dependent deductions, elderly household deductions, disability deductions

4.2 Property Eligibility

Geographic Requirements

  • Property must be in a USDA-designated "rural" area
  • Population thresholds:
  • 10,000 or less: Eligible
  • 10,001-20,000: Eligible if NOT in a Metropolitan Statistical Area (MSA)
  • 20,001-35,000: Eligible only if previously classified as rural (1990, 2000, or 2010 Census) AND has a serious lack of affordable mortgage options
  • Coverage: Approximately 74% of U.S. land area qualifies as rural
  • Eligibility map updated approximately every 5 years
  • Use official USDA Property Eligibility Map at eligibility.sc.egov.usda.gov

Property Requirements

  • Must be primary residence (owner-occupied)
  • Single-family homes, condos, modular homes, and certain manufactured homes
  • No income-producing farms or properties
  • Must meet USDA minimum property standards (safety, soundness, livability)
  • USDA appraisal required (similar to FHA appraisal requirements)

Surprising Eligibility

  • Many suburban areas and small towns qualify
  • Areas just outside major metro boundaries often qualify
  • Some neighborhoods within larger communities may qualify

4.3 DTI Limits

| Ratio Type | Standard Limit | With Compensating Factors | |-----------|---------------|--------------------------| | Front-end (housing) | 29% | Up to 32% | | Back-end (total debt) | 41% | Up to 44% (some lenders up to 46%) |

Direct Loans

  • Low-income households: 33% front-end maximum
  • Very-low income households: 29% front-end maximum
  • Back-end: 41% maximum

USDA Compensating Factors

  • Strong credit history (no lates in 12 months)
  • Stable employment (2+ years same employer)
  • Cash reserves after closing
  • Minimal increase in housing payment
  • Conservative debt usage
  • Demonstrated ability to manage comparable housing expenses

4.4 Additional USDA Requirements

  • No down payment required (100% financing)
  • Guarantee fee: 1.0% upfront (can be financed) + 0.35% annual (monthly payments)
  • Credit score: 640+ for automated approval; below 640 requires manual underwriting
  • No loan limits (based on borrower's qualifying ability and property value)
  • Occupancy: Primary residence only
  • First-time homebuyer: NOT required (existing homeowners can qualify if selling current home)
  • No liquid asset test: However, borrowers with significant liquid assets may be ineligible (shows they don't need the rural housing subsidy)

5. NON-QM / PORTFOLIO

5.1 Bank Statement Loans

Overview

  • Qualifies borrowers using 12-24 months of personal or business bank statements instead of tax returns
  • Ideal for self-employed borrowers who maximize tax deductions (reducing AGI)

Requirements

| Parameter | Typical Range | |-----------|--------------| | Minimum credit score | 620-680 | | Down payment | 10-25% | | Maximum LTV | 75-90% | | Reserves | 6-12 months PITI | | Maximum loan amount | $1M-$5M+ (varies by lender) | | DTI limits | Up to 50% |

Income Calculation

  • Personal bank statements (12-24 months): Average monthly deposits
  • Business bank statements (12-24 months): Average monthly deposits x expense factor (typically 50-70% of deposits counted as income)
  • Expense factor varies by industry and lender
  • CPA letter may strengthen the file

Documentation

  • 12 or 24 months consecutive bank statements (all pages)
  • CPA letter or profit/loss statement (some lenders)
  • Business license or proof of self-employment (2+ years)
  • Standard property documentation (appraisal, title, insurance)

5.2 DSCR (Debt Service Coverage Ratio) Loans

Overview

  • Qualifies based on the PROPERTY's income, not the BORROWER's income
  • No personal income documentation required
  • Ideal for real estate investors

DSCR Calculation

DSCR = Monthly Rental Income / Monthly PITIA (Principal + Interest + Taxes + Insurance + HOA)
  • DSCR of 1.0 = rental income covers the mortgage payment exactly
  • DSCR of 1.25 = rental income is 125% of the mortgage payment

Requirements

| Parameter | Typical Range | |-----------|--------------| | Minimum DSCR ratio | 0.75-1.25 (many lenders now accept 1.0) | | Minimum credit score | 620-680 (most mainstream: 680) | | Down payment | 20-25% | | Maximum LTV | 75-80% | | Reserves | 3-6 months PITI | | Interest rates | 6.5%-8.0%+ (0.5-1.5% above conventional) | | Maximum loan amount | $1M-$5M+ |

DSCR Tiers and Pricing

| DSCR Ratio | LTV Available | Rate Impact | |-----------|--------------|-------------| | 1.25+ | Up to 80% | Best pricing | | 1.0-1.24 | Up to 75% | Standard pricing | | 0.75-0.99 | Up to 70% | Higher rate (+0.5-1.0%) | | Below 0.75 | Limited availability | Significant rate premium |

Property Types

  • Single-family rentals (SFR)
  • 2-4 unit residential
  • Condos and townhomes
  • Short-term rentals (Airbnb/VRBO) - some lenders
  • Small commercial and mixed-use (expanding in 2026)
  • 5-10+ unit small multifamily (emerging programs)

Key Benefits

  • No personal income verification
  • No DTI calculation
  • No employment verification
  • Unlimited number of properties (most programs)
  • Can be held in LLC or entity name

5.3 Asset Depletion

Overview

Converts liquid assets into "monthly income" for qualification purposes, even if the borrower has little or no traditional income.

Calculation Formula

Monthly Qualifying Income = (Total Eligible Assets - Down Payment - Closing Costs - Reserves) / Remaining Loan Term in Months

Asset Discounting Rules

| Asset Type | Discount Applied | |-----------|-----------------| | Cash, checking, savings, money market | 100% of balance | | Stocks, bonds, mutual funds | 70-80% of market value | | Retirement accounts (IRA, 401k) - Age 59.5+ | 60-70% of vested balance | | Retirement accounts (IRA, 401k) - Under 59.5 | 50-60% of vested balance (penalty factored) | | Crypto assets | 50-70% (limited lenders accept) |

Loan Term Impact

| Loan Term | Divisor | Income Effect | |-----------|---------|--------------| | 30-year | 360 months | Lowest monthly income calculation | | 25-year | 300 months | Moderate boost | | 20-year | 240 months | Highest monthly income calculation |

Example Calculation

  • Total eligible assets: $2,000,000
  • Down payment: $200,000
  • Closing costs: $15,000
  • Required reserves: $50,000
  • Net available: $1,735,000
  • 30-year term: $1,735,000 / 360 = $4,819/month qualifying income

Typical Requirements

  • Minimum credit score: 680-720
  • Down payment: 20-30%
  • Assets must be seasoned (2+ months in borrower's accounts)
  • Reserves: 12-24 months PITI (beyond assets used for depletion calculation)

5.4 Foreign National Programs

Overview

Designed for non-U.S. citizens purchasing property in the United States who may lack SSN, U.S. credit history, or green card.

Requirements

| Parameter | Typical Range | |-----------|--------------| | Down payment | 25-35% | | Maximum LTV | 65-75% | | Minimum credit score | No U.S. score required; international credit report or bank references accepted | | Maximum loan amount | $1M-$3M+ | | Interest rates | 7.5-10%+ | | Property types | Primary, second home, investment |

Documentation (No SSN Pathway)

  • Valid passport (and visa if applicable)
  • International credit report or bank reference letters (showing on-time payments)
  • Foreign bank statements (12-24 months)
  • Employment contracts or business documentation
  • Income verification via translated tax returns, bank records
  • Proof of foreign residency

ITIN Loan Alternative

  • For borrowers with Individual Taxpayer Identification Number (ITIN) instead of SSN
  • Lower down payments possible (15-25%)
  • U.S.-based income documentation may be available
  • 2-year U.S. tax filing history strengthens the file

Key Considerations

  • FIRPTA withholding (15% of sale price for foreign sellers)
  • Property insurance may be more expensive
  • Some states have additional requirements for foreign ownership
  • Limited to specific lenders (not all offer foreign national programs)

5.5 Recent Credit Event Programs

Overview

Non-QM programs that dramatically reduce or eliminate waiting periods after bankruptcy, foreclosure, short sale, or other derogatory credit events.

Waiting Periods (Non-QM vs. Agency)

| Credit Event | Conventional | FHA | VA | Non-QM | |-------------|-------------|-----|----|----| | Chapter 7 Bankruptcy | 4 years | 2 years | 2 years | 1 day - 2 years | | Chapter 13 Bankruptcy | 2-4 years | 1 year (in plan) | 1 year (in plan) | 1 day - 1 year | | Foreclosure | 7 years | 3 years | 2 years | 1 day - 2 years | | Short Sale | 4 years | 3 years | 2 years | 1 day - 2 years | | Deed-in-Lieu | 4 years | 3 years | 2 years | 1 day - 2 years |

Non-QM Recent Credit Event Requirements

| Seasoning | Down Payment | Credit Score | Rate Impact | |-----------|-------------|-------------|-------------| | 1 day - 12 months | 25-30% | 660+ | Significant premium (+2-4%) | | 12-24 months | 20-25% | 640+ | Moderate premium (+1-2%) | | 24-48 months | 15-20% | 620+ | Modest premium (+0.5-1%) |

Additional Requirements

  • 12 months of clean payment history (0x30x12 — no 30-day lates in most recent 12 months)
  • Explanation letter for the credit event
  • Documentation of recovery (re-established credit)
  • 6-12 months reserves
  • Not all lenders offer these programs — non-QM guidelines vary significantly by investor

6. KEY EXCEPTION SCENARIOS (The YES Where Everyone Else Says NO)

6.1 High DTI with Compensating Factors

The Problem

Borrower's DTI exceeds standard limits (45% conventional, 43% FHA standard).

Solutions by Program

| Program | Standard DTI | Maximum DTI with Exceptions | How to Get There | |---------|-------------|---------------------------|-----------------| | Conventional (DU) | 45% | 50% | Strong AUS approval with reserves, credit, equity | | Conventional (Manual) | 36% | 50% | 2+ compensating factors, 6+ months reserves | | FHA (TOTAL) | 43% | 56.99% | AUS approval; strong credit + housing history | | FHA (Manual) | 43% | 50% | 2+ compensating factors per DTI matrix | | VA | 41% guideline | No cap | Residual income passes; explain DTI in narrative | | Non-QM | Varies | 55%+ | Lender-specific; strong reserves + credit |

Strongest Compensating Factors for High DTI

  1. Reserves: 6-12+ months of mortgage payments in liquid assets
  2. Low payment shock: New payment is same or less than current housing cost
  3. Excellent credit: 740+ score with clean 12-month payment history
  4. Large down payment: 20%+ from own funds
  5. Residual income: Well above VA or internal minimums
  6. Non-borrower household income: (HomeReady only)
  7. Stable employment: 5+ years same employer with rising income trajectory

6.2 Self-Employed with Declining Income

The Problem

Self-employed borrower shows year-over-year income decline on tax returns, causing underwriter to question income stability.

Solutions

  1. Use most recent year only: If decline is > 20%, FHA and conventional allow using most recent year's lower income (honest but sometimes workable if recent year is still sufficient)
  2. Manual underwriting: FHA manual underwrite allows more nuanced income analysis with compensating factors
  3. Bank statement loan (Non-QM): Use 12-24 months of bank deposits instead of tax returns; may show higher actual cash flow than tax returns reflect
  4. Interim financial statements: If current year is trending UP from the declined year, provide year-to-date profit/loss and bank statements showing recovery
  5. CPA letter: Accountant letter explaining the decline (one-time event, industry cycle, COVID recovery, business investment) with projection of normalized income
  6. Add co-borrower: Non-occupant co-borrower (if FHA) or co-borrower with W-2 income to supplement
  7. Wait and refile: If possible, delay application until next tax filing shows recovery

Documentation for Declining Income

  • 2 years personal + business returns
  • YTD profit/loss statement
  • 3 months business bank statements
  • CPA letter with explanation
  • Additional reserves: 3 months PITI (1-2 units), 6 months (3-4 units)

6.3 Multiple Financed Properties

The Problem

Investor owns multiple financed properties and needs another mortgage.

Solutions by Number of Properties

| Properties Financed | Conventional (Fannie) | DSCR (Non-QM) | Portfolio | |--------------------|----------------------|---------------|----------| | 1-4 | Standard guidelines | Available | Available | | 5-10 | Fannie Mae 5-10 program | Available | Available | | 11+ | Not available | Available (unlimited) | Available |

Fannie Mae 5-10 Financed Properties Requirements

  • 25% down payment (1-unit) or 30% (2-4 unit)
  • 6 months reserves on ALL financed properties
  • 2 years income tax returns
  • All rental income documented
  • 7-year waiting period after bankruptcy/foreclosure (extended from standard)
  • 720+ credit score (lender overlay common)

DSCR Solution for 10+ Properties

  • No limit on number of financed properties
  • Each property qualifies independently based on rental income vs. PITIA
  • No personal income verification
  • Can be held in LLC name
  • Rates: 6.5-8.5%+

6.4 Non-Occupant Co-Borrower

FHA Non-Occupant Co-Borrower Rules

  • At least one borrower must occupy as primary residence
  • Family member co-borrower: 3.5% down payment allowed
  • Non-family co-borrower: 25% down payment required
  • Acceptable family members: Parent, grandparent, sibling, child, spouse, in-laws
  • Up to 4 total borrowers on FHA loan
  • Co-borrower's income and debts are both included in qualification
  • Uses lower of the two median credit scores
  • Co-borrower is on the mortgage note and title (legally responsible)
  • Co-borrower's ability to qualify for their OWN mortgage is impacted until 12 months of payment history established by primary borrower

Conventional Non-Occupant Co-Borrower Rules

  • Occupying borrower must contribute minimum 5% down payment from own funds (if co-borrower is used to qualify)
  • If LTV is 80% or less: No minimum borrower contribution required
  • Both borrowers' credit, income, and debts evaluated

VA Non-Occupant Co-Borrower

  • VA allows a co-borrower who is a spouse or another eligible veteran
  • Non-veteran, non-spouse co-borrowers: VA guaranty only covers the veteran's portion; the non-guaranteed portion requires a down payment

6.5 Student Loan IBR vs. Standard Payment

The Challenge

Borrower has large student loan balance ($100,000+) with a $0 or low IBR payment. Different programs calculate this differently, dramatically affecting DTI.

Program Comparison for $100,000 Student Loan Balance with $0 IBR Payment

| Program | Monthly Payment Used | Impact on DTI | |---------|---------------------|---------------| | FHA | $500 (0.5% of balance) | Moderate | | Fannie Mae (DU) | $0 if $0 reported on credit report; else 1% ($1,000) | Low or High | | Freddie Mac | $500 (0.5% of balance) if $0 or not reported | Moderate | | VA | Actual $0 payment accepted | Minimal |

Strategies to Minimize Student Loan DTI Impact

  1. Get IBR payment documented: Obtain loan servicer letter showing actual monthly payment amount
  2. Request fully amortized payment letter: If the fully amortized payment over remaining term is LOWER than the calculated percentage, use that instead
  3. Choose the right program: VA is most favorable (uses actual $0); FHA improved significantly with ML 2021-13 (0.5% vs old 1%)
  4. Consolidate student loans: May establish a documented payment that's lower than percentage calculations
  5. Pay down principal: Reducing the balance directly reduces the calculated payment (0.5% or 1% of a lower balance)
  6. Program switch: If conventional is calculating at 1%, consider FHA (0.5%) or VA ($0)

6.6 Gift Funds Rules by Program

| Program | Down Payment from Gifts | Closing Costs from Gifts | Who Can Gift | |---------|------------------------|--------------------------|-------------| | Conventional (1-unit primary) | 100% allowed | Allowed | Family, fiance, employer, church, nonprofit | | Conventional (multi-unit/2nd home) | Must contribute 5% own funds | Allowed | Family, fiance, employer, church, nonprofit | | Conventional (investment) | Generally not allowed | Limited | N/A | | FHA | 100% allowed | Allowed | Family, fiance, employer, charity, government, trusts | | VA | N/A ($0 down) | Allowed | Anyone (no restrictions on donor) | | USDA | N/A ($0 down) | Allowed | Family, charitable organizations |

Gift Documentation Required (All Programs)

  1. Gift letter signed by donor stating:
  • Donor name and relationship to borrower
  • Dollar amount of gift
  • Address of property being purchased
  • Statement that NO repayment is expected or required
  1. Proof of donor's ability to give (donor bank statement showing funds)
  2. Proof of transfer (wire confirmation, deposit receipt, or bank statements showing withdrawal and deposit)

6.7 Divorce Decree Situations

Income from Divorce Decree

To qualify as income (alimony, child support, separate maintenance):

  • Must be documented in court order or divorce decree
  • Must have 6 months of consistent receipt history (conventional); 3 months for VA
  • Must be expected to continue for at least 3 more years from closing
  • Lender verifies through bank statements, canceled checks, or court records

Debts from Divorce Decree

  • If divorce decree assigns a debt to the ex-spouse, conventional loans may exclude it from DTI IF:
  • 12 months of payment history by the ex-spouse is documented
  • Refinance out of the borrower's name has not yet occurred
  • FHA generally requires the debt to be included regardless of divorce decree
  • VA may exclude debts assigned to ex-spouse per divorce decree

Property from Divorce

  • Departing residence awarded to ex-spouse in divorce: Mortgage can be excluded from DTI if:
  • Documented in the divorce decree
  • Refinanced out of borrower's name, OR
  • 12 months payment history by ex-spouse documented (conventional)

6.8 Child Support / Alimony as Qualifying Income

Requirements to Use as Income

| Requirement | Conventional | FHA | VA | |------------|-------------|-----|-----| | Documentation | Court order/decree | Court order/decree | Court order/decree | | Receipt history | 6 months consistent | 6 months consistent | 3 months consistent | | Continuation requirement | 3 years from closing | 3 years from closing | 3 years from closing | | Verification method | Bank statements, checks | Bank statements, checks | Bank statements, checks |

Child Support vs. Alimony Distinctions

  • Child support: Not taxable income to recipient; not deductible by payor. Age of children determines 3-year continuation (child must be under ~15 at closing for 3-year continuance)
  • Alimony (post-12/31/2018 agreements): Not taxable to recipient; not deductible by payor
  • Alimony (pre-12/31/2018 agreements): Taxable to recipient; deductible by payor (unless agreement modified to new rules)

Voluntary Payments

  • Payments not required by court order CANNOT be used as qualifying income
  • Informal arrangements do not qualify regardless of consistency

6.9 Rental Income from Departing Residence

The Challenge

Borrower is buying a new primary residence and converting current home to a rental. How does the existing mortgage payment and rental income factor into qualification?

Fannie Mae Requirements

  1. Equity requirement: At least 30% equity in departing residence (verified by appraisal)
  2. Lease agreement: Fully executed current lease agreement required
  3. Rental income calculation:
  • Gross rental income from lease
  • Add 100% of qualifying rental income to borrower's total monthly income
  • Include full PITIA of departing residence in monthly debts (not netted)
  1. No rental management experience required (Fannie Mae only; Freddie Mac requires 1 year)
  2. Market rent verification: Form 1007 or comparable rent analysis to verify lease rate is market-supported

Documentation Options

  • Fully executed lease agreement with tenant signatures
  • Security deposit receipts and first month's rent documentation
  • Bank statements showing 2+ months of rental deposits (if lease already active)
  • Form 1007 (Single-Family Comparable Rent Schedule)

Alternative if < 30% Equity

  • If borrower has less than 30% equity, they generally cannot use rental income from the departing residence
  • Full PITIA of departing residence counts as a debt without offsetting rental income
  • Solution: Non-QM DSCR on the departing residence (refinance it as investment property separately)

6.10 Commission Income with Less Than 2 Years History

Conventional Options

  • 12-24 months history: May be accepted if:
  • Most recent tax return covers 12 months of commission income
  • Positive compensating factors (excellent credit, strong assets)
  • Commission income is likely to continue
  • Written justification from lender's underwriter

FHA Options (More Flexible)

  • 1 year minimum in same or similar line of work
  • Calculation: Lesser of 2-year average OR average over time earned
  • FHA is the preferred path for borrowers with 12-18 months commission history

Documentation

  • Tax returns for available years (1-2 years)
  • Most recent pay stubs showing commission breakdown
  • Written Verification of Employment (VOE) from employer confirming commission structure, history, and likelihood of continuance
  • Explanation of industry and compensation structure

Cannot Use Commission Income

  • Less than 12 months of receipt history (any program)
  • No documented likelihood of continuation
  • Highly volatile commission with no discernible pattern

7. COMMON DENIAL REASONS AND SOLUTIONS

7.1 DTI Too High

Solutions (Ranked by Effectiveness)

  1. Switch programs:
  • Conventional 45% max → FHA up to 56.99% (AUS) or 50% (manual)
  • FHA 43% max → FHA manual with compensating factors (50%)
  • Any program → VA (no hard DTI cap; residual income based)
  1. Add co-borrower: Non-occupant co-borrower (FHA allows family members) adds income to lower DTI
  2. Pay down debt: Eliminate or reduce revolving balances to lower monthly obligations
  3. Manual underwriting: FHA/VA manual with compensating factors (reserves, payment history, credit)
  4. Exclude debts: Divorce decree debts (12 months ex-spouse payment history), debts being paid by business (documented on Schedule C)
  5. Longer loan term: 30-year vs. 15-year reduces monthly payment
  6. Lower purchase price: Reduces proposed housing expense

7.2 Credit Score Too Low

Solutions by Score Range

| Current Score | Solution Path | |--------------|--------------| | 600-619 | Rapid rescore (pay down cards, dispute errors); target 620 for conventional | | 580-599 | FHA with 3.5% down; rapid rescore toward 620 | | 500-579 | FHA with 10% down (manual underwrite); limited lender availability | | Below 500 | Not FHA eligible; non-traditional credit path; credit repair program | | No credit score | Non-traditional credit (FHA manual UW with 3 trade lines, 12-month history) |

Rapid Rescore Process

  • Timeline: 2-5 business days (vs. 30-45 days for normal credit reporting)
  • Cost to borrower: $0 (lender pays; FCRA prohibits charging borrowers)
  • Common actions:
  • Pay down credit card balances below 30% utilization (ideally below 10%)
  • Dispute and remove inaccurate derogatory items
  • Have authorized user accounts added or updated
  • Remove collections that were paid but not updated
  • Typical score improvement: 20-40 points (can be 100+ if errors are removed)
  • Process: Lender identifies improvement → borrower provides documentation → lender submits to bureaus → new score generated in 2-5 days

Credit Score Threshold Impacts

| Score Threshold | What It Unlocks | |----------------|----------------| | 500 | FHA eligibility (10% down) | | 580 | FHA 3.5% down payment | | 620 | Conventional eligibility; USDA auto-approval | | 640 | Better LLPAs; most DSCR programs | | 660 | HomeReady manual UW; reduced LLPAs | | 680 | Better PMI rates; most Non-QM programs | | 700 | Significantly better LLPAs and PMI | | 720 | Near-best pricing on most programs | | 740 | Best conventional pricing tier | | 760 | Best LLPA tier | | 780+ | Zero LLPA on most transactions |

7.3 Self-Employed Income Insufficient

Solutions

  1. Bank statement loan (Non-QM): Average 12-24 months of deposits; typically shows higher income than tax returns for heavily-deducting business owners
  2. Proper income calculation: Ensure all allowable add-backs (depreciation, amortization, non-cash expenses) are included
  3. Fannie Mae Income Calculator: Use the official tool to ensure proper calculation methodology
  4. FHA: More lenient on commission/variable income (1-year history acceptable)
  5. Asset depletion: If business owner has significant assets but low reported income
  6. DSCR: For investment properties, bypass personal income entirely
  7. Co-borrower: Add W-2 income earner as co-borrower

Common Self-Employed Income Calculation Errors

  • Failing to add back depreciation
  • Using gross income instead of net
  • Not subtracting business use of home deduction that was already taken on Schedule A
  • Incorrectly averaging when income is declining (should use lower year)
  • Counting distributions as income (they're not; use business net income)

7.4 Too Many Financed Properties

Solutions

| Properties | Best Program Options | |-----------|---------------------| | 1-4 | All programs available (conventional, FHA, VA) | | 5-10 | Fannie Mae 5-10 program; DSCR; portfolio | | 11-20 | DSCR; portfolio; commercial | | 20+ | Commercial lending; DSCR; blanket loans |

DSCR Advantages for Investors

  • No limit on number of properties
  • No personal income verification
  • Each property qualifies independently
  • Can close in LLC/entity name
  • Scale indefinitely with positive cash-flowing properties

7.5 Recent Bankruptcy/Foreclosure

Solution Matrix
