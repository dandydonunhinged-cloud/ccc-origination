MARKET OVERVIEW

Wholesale Channel in 2025-2026

  • Broker market share reached 20.2% in Q4 2025
  • Broker volume rose ~17% in 2025
  • Non-QM originations finished ~$120B in 2025 with securitizations hitting $70B+
  • Fannie Mae eliminated 620 minimum credit score requirement effective Nov 16, 2025
  • VantageScore 4.0 now accepted alongside FICO by Fannie/Freddie (July 2025)
  • 2026 conforming loan limit: $819,000 (standard); higher in high-cost areas

HOW WHOLESALE RATE SHEETS WORK

Daily Pricing Cycle

  1. Wholesale lenders publish rate sheets each business day, typically by 8-10 AM ET
  2. Rates can be repriced intraday if MBS markets move significantly (mid-day reprices)
  3. Rate sheets are published as PDFs, through broker portals, and via pricing engines
  4. A rate sheet is a grid with interest rates down the left side and lock periods across the top (15, 30, 45, 60 days)

Par Rate

  • Par rate = the interest rate where neither the broker nor borrower pays/receives a premium
  • Expressed as 100.000 or 0.000 on the rate sheet
  • Below par (e.g., 99.500) = borrower must pay 0.5 points (discount points) to get that rate
  • Above par (e.g., 100.750) = lender pays broker 0.75 points premium (rebate)

Rate Sheet Structure

Rate    | 15-Day Lock | 30-Day Lock | 45-Day Lock | 60-Day Lock
--------|-------------|-------------|-------------|------------
7.000%  | 101.250     | 101.000     | 100.750     | 100.500
6.875%  | 100.875     | 100.625     | 100.375     | 100.125
6.750%  | 100.500     | 100.250     | 100.000     | 99.750     <-- Par at 30-day
6.625%  | 100.125     | 99.875      | 99.625      | 99.375
6.500%  |  99.750     | 99.500      | 99.250      | 99.000

Reading the Sheet

  • Numbers > 100.000 = rebate (lender pays broker; higher rate than par)
  • Numbers < 100.000 = cost (borrower pays discount points; lower rate than par)
  • Longer lock periods = slightly worse pricing (time value of money)

Price Adjustments Applied ON TOP of Base Rate

  • LLPAs (Loan Level Price Adjustments) -- from Fannie/Freddie based on risk factors
  • Credit score adjusters
  • LTV adjusters
  • Property type adjusters (investment, 2-4 unit, condo)
  • Loan purpose adjusters (cash-out worse than purchase)
  • Occupancy adjusters

BROKER COMPENSATION MODEL

Post-2010 Dodd-Frank Rules (TILA-RESPA)

Mortgage brokers must choose ONE compensation method per loan:

Option 1: Borrower-Paid Compensation

  • Borrower pays broker directly via origination fee or points
  • Broker originates at or near par rate (no lender rebate)
  • Disclosed on Loan Estimate and Closing Disclosure

Option 2: Lender-Paid Compensation

  • Lender pays broker via Yield Spread Premium (YSP) / rebate
  • Broker originates at above-par rate; lender pays the spread to the broker
  • Borrower pays no origination fee but gets a slightly higher rate
  • Maximum YSP typically ~2.75% of loan amount
  • Must be fully disclosed

CANNOT DO BOTH

  • A broker cannot charge the borrower an origination fee AND collect YSP from the lender
  • This was the key Dodd-Frank reform

Servicing Released Premium (SRP)

  • SRP is paid to the lender (not the broker) when they sell servicing rights
  • Banks/direct lenders get this when they sell your loan after closing
  • Federal law does NOT require SRP disclosure
  • Brokers do not participate in SRP

LOAN LEVEL PRICE ADJUSTMENTS (LLPAs)

What They Are

LLPAs are risk-based pricing adjustments set by FHFA and applied by Fannie Mae and Freddie Mac to conventional mortgages. They are added to (or subtracted from) the base rate based on loan risk characteristics.

Key LLPA Factors

| Factor | Impact | |--------|--------| | Credit Score | Lower score = higher LLPA (worse pricing) | | LTV Ratio | Higher LTV = higher LLPA | | Loan Purpose | Cash-out refi > rate/term refi > purchase | | Occupancy | Investment > second home > primary | | Property Type | Condo/manufactured > 2-4 unit > SFR | | Subordinate Financing | Additional LLPA if second lien exists | | First-Time Buyer | Favorable LLPA adjustments for qualifying FTBs |

Sweet Spots for Best Pricing

  • Best absolute rate: 40% down payment with any credit score above 640, OR 25% down with 780+ credit score
  • Credit score and LTV interact -- a 780 FICO at 95% LTV still gets hit; a 680 FICO at 60% LTV is much better

Fannie Mae LLPA Matrix

Available at: singlefamily.fanniemae.com/media/9391/display

2025-2026 Changes

  • Fannie Mae eliminated 620 minimum credit score (Nov 2025)
  • VantageScore 4.0 now accepted alongside FICO (July 2025)
  • Some wholesale lenders may add their own LLPAs ON TOP of agency LLPAs (overlays)

BROKER APPROVAL PROCESS

Typical Steps to Get Approved with a Wholesale Lender

1. Obtain State License

  • NMLS registration required
  • Pass SAFE Mortgage Loan Originator exam (National + State components; 75% passing)
  • Complete pre-licensing education (23 hours in Texas)
  • Background check (FBI fingerprints)
  • Surety bond (varies by state)

2. Company Setup

  • Establish mortgage brokerage entity (LLC, Corp, etc.)
  • Obtain company NMLS number
  • E&O (Errors & Omissions) insurance -- generally not required for brokers at the same level as bankers, but some wholesale lenders require it
  • Quality Control plan (basic elements: application quality, compliance audit, fraud red flags)

3. Apply to Wholesale Lenders

Typical documentation required:

  • Company financials (most recent)
  • Ownership and organizational information
  • Key personnel resumes and NMLS IDs
  • Credit reports on principals
  • Copy of state license(s)
  • E&O insurance certificate (if required)
  • Surety bond confirmation
  • Quality Control plan
  • Business plan / production history (some lenders)

4. Account Setup

  • Sign broker agreement / TPO agreement
  • Get portal access credentials
  • Account Executive assigned
  • Pricing engine setup (LoanSifter, Lender Price, etc.)
  • Training on lender-specific submission requirements

5. Ongoing Requirements

  • Continuing education (state-specific annual hours)
  • Annual license renewal
  • Maintain E&O insurance and surety bond
  • Comply with lender-specific quality requirements

Typical Timeline

  • State licensing: 30-90 days
  • Individual lender approval: 1-4 weeks per lender
  • Most brokers apply to 5-15 wholesale lenders to ensure product coverage and competitive pricing

Licensing Authority

Requirements

| Requirement | Detail | |-------------|--------| | Pre-licensing Education | 23 hours NMLS-approved courses | | Exam | SAFE MLO exam -- National + Texas (UST) components; 75% passing | | Surety Bond | $50,000 minimum | | Net Assets | Minimum $25,000 (alternative to surety bond) | | Background Check | FBI fingerprint-based criminal background check | | Application | Online through NMLS | | Continuing Education | Annual requirement (state-specific hours) | | Surety Bond (Loan Servicer add-on) | $25,000 if servicing < $25M/year; $50,000 if > $25M/year |

Key Texas Considerations

  • Texas has unique homestead and lien laws that affect lending
  • Cash-out refinances in Texas are governed by the Texas Constitution (Section 50(a)(6))
  • Texas "equity loans" (cash-out refis) have specific restrictions:
  • Max 80% LTV
  • Closing costs cannot exceed 2% of the loan amount in certain fee categories
  • 12-day cooling-off period
  • Special disclosure requirements
  • These are state constitutional requirements that apply regardless of loan type
  • Wholesale lenders must be specifically licensed/approved to lend in Texas

BUILDING A PROGRAMMATIC RATE COMPARISON SYSTEM

Architecture for an AI-Powered Loan Matching Engine

Data Layer: Rate Sheet Ingestion

Option 1: Pricing Engine API Integration (Recommended)

  • Subscribe to Optimal Blue/LoanSifter API -- covers 120+ wholesale investors
  • Or Lender Price Marketplace API -- AI-driven, growing lender network
  • Or Mortech API -- hundreds of investors, tens of millions of daily requests
  • These engines already normalize rate data across lenders
  • API returns: eligible products, rates, points, LLPAs, and lock options for any borrower scenario

Option 2: Direct Lender API Integration

  • Some lenders offer direct pricing APIs (UWM EASE, Rocket Pro, etc.)
  • More work to integrate but gives you raw lender data
  • Must normalize data across different formats

Option 3: Rate Sheet Scraping (Last Resort)

  • Parse PDF rate sheets from daily emails
  • OCR + structured extraction
  • High maintenance; format changes break parsers
  • Not recommended as primary approach

Logic Layer: Loan Matching Engine

Input: Borrower Profile
  - Credit score (FICO)
  - Income type (W2, self-employed, 1099, etc.)
  - DTI ratio
  - Down payment / LTV
  - Property type (SFR, condo, 2-4 unit, manufactured)
  - Occupancy (primary, second home, investment)
  - Loan purpose (purchase, rate/term refi, cash-out refi)
  - Loan amount
  - State (Texas-specific rules)
  - Special factors (ITIN, foreign national, recent credit event, etc.)

Processing:
  1. Filter: Which lenders' programs is borrower eligible for?
  2. Price: What is the all-in rate after LLPAs and overlays?
  3. Rank: Sort by total cost (rate + points + fees)
  4. Present: Show top options with trade-offs

Output: Ranked Lender Recommendations
  - Lender name
  - Program name
  - Interest rate
  - Points/credits
  - Estimated total closing costs
  - Lock period options
  - Key program features/restrictions
  - Broker compensation at each rate tier

Key API Options

| Platform | API Type | Coverage | Cost Model | Best For | |----------|----------|----------|------------|----------| | Optimal Blue/LoanSifter | REST API | 120+ wholesale investors | Subscription | Broker-focused comparison | | Lender Price | REST API | Growing marketplace | Free for brokers (lenders pay) | AI-driven matching | | Mortech | REST API | Hundreds of investors | Subscription | High-volume rate queries | | Polly | REST API | Hundreds of investors | Subscription | Lock desk automation | | Fannie Mae Loan Pricing API | REST API | Fannie Mae only | Free (for approved sellers) | Agency pricing/LLPA calculation |

Implementation Recommendations

  1. Start with LoanSifter (Optimal Blue) -- most brokers already use it; API is mature; 120+ investors
  2. Supplement with Lender Price Marketplace -- free for brokers; growing fast; AI-driven
  3. Add ARIVE integration -- if using ARIVE as LOS, pricing is built in with 22+ lenders
  4. Build borrower profile intake -- structured form that captures all matching criteria
  5. Create scoring algorithm -- weight factors by borrower priority (lowest rate vs. lowest closing costs vs. fastest close vs. most flexible guidelines)
  6. Texas rules engine -- hardcode Texas constitutional requirements (80% max cash-out, etc.)
  7. Non-QM routing logic -- if borrower doesn't fit agency, automatically route to non-QM specialists (Angel Oak, A&D, Deephaven, etc.)

Technology Stack Suggestion

  • Backend: Python (FastAPI or Django) for API orchestration
  • Database: PostgreSQL for borrower profiles, rate history, lender guidelines
  • Caching: Redis for rate sheet caching (rates change intraday)
  • Frontend: React or Next.js for broker-facing dashboard
  • ML Layer: Scoring model trained on historical loan-to-lender matching outcomes

KEY TAKEAWAYS FOR AI-POWERED LOAN MATCHING

  1. No single lender covers everything. You need a matrix of 8-12 lenders minimum to cover all borrower profiles.
  1. Critical routing logic:
  • Standard W2 borrower, 620+ FICO, agency-eligible --> UWM, Rocket, Pennymac, Freedom (compare rates)
  • Self-employed, needs bank statements --> Angel Oak, A&D, NewRez SmartSelf, Arc Home, UWM Bank Statement
  • Real estate investor (DSCR) --> Angel Oak, A&D, Arc Home, UWM Investor Flex, NewRez SmartVest
  • Credit-challenged (sub-580) --> Carrington (FHA/VA at 500)
  • Foreign national --> A&D Mortgage (DSCR foreign national; no US credit needed)
  • ITIN borrower --> A&D Mortgage, Deephaven, FNBA
  • Recent credit event --> Arc Home Clean Slate, NewRez SmartEdge, Angel Oak Portfolio Select
  • Asset-rich / retiree --> Angel Oak Asset Qualifier, Arc Home Asset Utilization, Pennymac Asset Depletion
  1. Texas-specific: Always check 80% max LTV on cash-out refis (constitutional requirement), 2% fee cap, and 12-day cooling period.
  1. Technology path: Start with LoanSifter API (Optimal Blue) for 120+ investors, supplement with Lender Price Marketplace (free), and build borrower profile routing on top.
  1. Non-QM is booming: $120B+ in 2025 originations. Every major wholesale lender is adding non-QM products. Angel Oak and A&D Mortgage are the current non-QM leaders.
