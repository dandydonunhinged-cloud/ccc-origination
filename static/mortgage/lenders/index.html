PHASE 5: LENDER SUBMISSION — Full Operational Build-Out

Package the file, submit to the right lender, maximize your comp

SUBMISSION DECISION MATRIX

How to pick the lender (priority order):

  1. Best fit for the deal — does the borrower qualify cleanly?
  2. Best comp for you — when two lenders are equally good, pick the one that pays more
  3. Fastest close — if the borrower has a deadline, speed wins
  4. AE relationship — lenders you have a relationship with move your files faster
  5. Fallback plan — always have lender #2 ready in case #1 falls apart

The RPCCP admin report already ranks lenders by these factors. Use it.

SUBMISSION WORKFLOW

Step 1: Select lenders (5 minutes)

  • Review RPCCP admin report
  • Pick primary lender (best fit + best comp)
  • Pick backup lender (different approval criteria in case primary doesn't work)
  • Note the comp estimate for each

Step 2: Package the file (15-30 minutes)

  • Login to primary lender's portal
  • Create new loan application
  • Enter borrower/entity info
  • Enter property info
  • Enter deal structure (purchase price, down payment, loan amount)
  • Upload all documents:
  • Purchase contract
  • Lease / rent schedule
  • Bank statements
  • Entity docs
  • Insurance
  • ID
  • Any deal-specific docs (SOW for flip, plans for construction, etc.)
  • Review for completeness — missing docs = delays

Step 3: Submit and confirm

  • Hit submit in the portal
  • Note the loan number / reference number
  • Screenshot the confirmation
  • Save to deal folder: deals/[borrower]/lender_submissions/

Step 4: Notify the AE (optional but recommended)

If you have a relationship with the AE:

"Hey [AE name], just submitted [borrower last name] through the portal — loan #[number]. [Loan type] on a [property] in [state]. Clean file, [credit score], [DSCR ratio]. Let me know if you need anything."

This gets your file looked at faster. Relationships matter.

Step 5: Update your pipeline tracker

Step 6: Tell the borrower

"Hey [Name], good news — I've submitted your deal to our lending partner. They'll review it and order the appraisal within 1-2 business days. I'll keep you updated on every step. Expected close: [date]."

DUAL SUBMISSION STRATEGY

When to submit to two lenders simultaneously:

  1. Tight deadline — borrower needs to close in 14 days, can't afford a fail
  2. Borderline qualification — primary lender might say no on appraisal or credit
  3. Rate shopping — submit to two, compare term sheets, pick the winner
  4. Complex deal — different lenders may structure it differently

When NOT to dual-submit:

  1. Clean, straightforward DSCR deal with strong borrower — one lender is enough
  2. The borrower doesn't want multiple credit pulls (each lender pulls separately)

How to manage dual submissions:

  • Submit to both portals same day
  • Track both in your pipeline
  • When the first term sheet comes back, compare to the second
  • Accept the better offer, withdraw the other
  • NEVER let two lenders get to closing on the same deal

COMP NEGOTIATION

Most broker comp is set by the lender's comp schedule. But you CAN optimize:

  1. Choose the higher-YSP lender when two lenders offer similar rates
  • Lender A: 7.375% rate, 1.0% YSP → you get $3,000 on a $300K loan
  • Lender B: 7.375% rate, 1.75% YSP → you get $5,250 on the same loan
  • Same rate to the borrower, 75% more comp to you
  1. Price up the borrower-paid points (within reason)
  • Standard: 1 point
  • On complex deals or fast closes: 1.5-2 points
  • On SBA: up to 5% (SBA cap)
  • Always disclose in your broker fee agreement
  1. Stack ancillary fees
  • Processing: $995
  • Admin/UW: $500
  • Doc prep: $250 (if applicable)
  • Total: $1,745 per deal on top of comp
  1. Negotiate with smaller/newer lenders
  • Private lenders and debt funds often have negotiable comp
  • If you bring consistent volume, you can negotiate higher YSP or volume bonuses

BROKER FEE AGREEMENT — SIGN BEFORE SUBMITTING

When to get it signed:

  • After the borrower says "let's move forward" (Phase 3)
  • Before you submit to any lender (Phase 5)
  • NEVER submit a file without a signed fee agreement

Template:

BROKER FEE AGREEMENT — BUSINESS PURPOSE LOAN

This agreement is between:
  Borrower: [Full Legal Name / Entity Name]
  Broker: Don Brown / Click Click Close

1. SERVICES: Broker will assist Borrower in obtaining a business purpose
   loan for the acquisition/refinance of investment/commercial real property.

2. BROKER COMPENSATION:

      compensation from the lender, which does not increase Borrower's rate.

3. BUSINESS PURPOSE CONFIRMATION: Borrower confirms that this loan is for
   business or investment purposes and NOT for personal, family, or household
   use. The property will NOT be Borrower's primary residence.

4. NO GUARANTEE: Broker does not guarantee loan approval, terms, or rates.
   Loan approval is subject to lender underwriting, appraisal, and other factors.

5. EXCLUSIVITY: Borrower agrees to work exclusively with Broker for this
   transaction for a period of 90 days from the date of this agreement.

6. TERMINATION: Either party may terminate this agreement with written notice.
   If the loan closes within 90 days of termination, the fee is still owed.

7. GOVERNING LAW: This agreement is governed by the laws of the State of Texas.

Signed:
_________________________ Date: _________
[Borrower Name/Entity]

_________________________ Date: _________
Don Brown / Click Click Close

Notes:

  • This is a BUSINESS PURPOSE loan — RESPA/TILA don't apply
  • No GFE or Loan Estimate required
  • No Closing Disclosure required (though some lenders provide one anyway)
  • The fee agreement IS your disclosure — keep it clear and simple
  • Have a lawyer review this template before using (one-time cost)
